# A developer's wallcovering tender and handover checklist

What a wallcovering package needs to be priced, programmed and handed over: scope, measurement basis, substrate handover, programme, changes, snagging and spares.

By [Dorin Burcus](https://www.mrwallcover.com/about/#dorin), founder · Last updated 18 October 2026

Can this wallcovering package be procured, coordinated and handed over predictably? It can when the tender defines seven things: the scope, the measurement basis, the substrate handover, the programme, how changes are handled, how snagging is closed and what spares are kept. A package becomes difficult when decisions, materials and site readiness arrive in the wrong order. This checklist puts them in order, and the blank [package schedule](https://www.mrwallcover.com/downloads/wallcovering-package-schedule.csv) records them room by room.

## Scope: what is in the package?

List every area with its material reference, drawing revision, quantity basis and required date. State whether the package is wallcovering only or wallcovering within a decorating package; at [Brown’s Hotel](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/browns-hotel-mayfair/) and [15 Old Bailey](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/old-bailey-hotel/) Mr Wallcover was main decorating contractor, while at [The OWO](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/raffles-london-the-owo/) the wallcovering package stood alone. Say whether preparation, lining, primers, paper on joinery, protection, access equipment and waste removal are included, so quotations can be compared on the same basis. Our guide to [what changes the cost](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/installation-cost/) explains each line.

## Measurement basis: what is being priced?

State whether areas are measured net of openings, whether returns, reveals and joinery faces are measured separately, and whether the contractor or the client confirms the purchase quantity. The labour measure and the order quantity are different numbers: the order has to cover full drops, the repeat and the batch. The allowance is typically 15–30%, depending on the pattern repeat and the layout, with staircases at the high end (around 30%) because of the angles. Extra rolls for pattern matching are confirmed at survey. See the [quantities page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/quantities/).

## Substrate handover: when is a wall “ready”?

Define ready for the specified product: sound, dry to a stated reading, filled and sanded to an agreed standard, primed or not, lined or not. Name who inspects, how defects are recorded and how unresolved preparation affects the programme and the price. Period walls and new plaster both carry risk; our [wall preparation guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wall-preparation/) lists what has to be checked, and the survey is a judgement on the wall as well as a measure.

## Programme: what has to finish first?

Sequence wallcovering after wet trades, first-fix joinery and ceiling finishes, and before second-fix items that would damage a finished wall. Show target dates per area, the working hours and access rules, and whether the building is occupied. Delivery of free-issue goods is timed to the hang and paper is not stored in corridors; where the site is a working hotel, the [hotel specification guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/hotel-wallcovering-specification/) covers room release and phasing. A tender deadline is not the requested start date.

## Lead times: when should the installer be involved?

Earlier than the start date. A start date is given after the survey, and lead times depend on the project; when a client or contractor needs support quickly, the team can sometimes mobilise within two to three days. If the wallcovering is free-issue, the supplier’s lead time sits inside the programme and the hang is planned around the delivery, so put the order date on the schedule as well as the hang date. For a scenic paper or a mural, bring the installer in before the dimensions go to production, because the panels are made to the finished walls; skirting height, cornice depth and a new lining all change them. On a tender, state both the tender deadline and the requested start date.

## Site readiness: what has to be true on the day?

Agree a short list for each area before the hang is booked: wet trades finished and the walls dry to the agreed reading; filling, lining and drying time already in the programme rather than discovered on the day the material arrives; and first-fix joinery and ceiling finishes complete. Free-issue deliveries are checked against the measure when they arrive, and scenic panels are checked dry against the maker’s layout before any paste and before anything is cut, so a sequence, sizing or print problem comes to light while it is still easy to resolve. Our [set-out and pattern matching guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/set-out-and-pattern-matching/) explains why a pronounced pattern needs its set-out agreed before cutting starts.

## Changes: how are substitutions and variations handled?

Decide who approves a substitute product, and require appearance, installation implications and the supporting documents to be reviewed before approval. Where the project carries fire, acoustic or environmental requirements, obtain the document for the exact product and have the responsible project professional accept it; a family name on a schedule is not evidence. Keep a dated revision on the schedule and the affected elevations, and agree how additional preparation is authorised once walls are opened up.

## Sample area and snagging: how is acceptance recorded?

Agree a sample area or mock-up before the repeating rooms start, in the permanent lighting where possible, record its acceptance and measure every later room against it. Agree who protects finished walls from following trades, how snags are recorded, and what counts as a material characteristic rather than a defect: visible seams and shading in natural weaves, for instance, are the material and should be settled at the sample stage. The [designer checklist](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/designer-specification-checklist/) covers the design side of that record.

## Spares and handover: what does the client keep?

Hand over a room-by-room record of product, colourway, batch and care sheet, together with spare material from the same batch, stored as the maker recommends. State the limits of a later repair, since a different batch will show. The handover pack should also say what aftercare is included; our [aftercare page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/aftercare/) sets that out for the work we deliver.

## When does this checklist stop being enough?

It defines the package; it does not certify compliance, insurance or contract terms, which are confirmed with current documents for the specific project. For a tender, send the drawing revisions, the room and material schedules, areas and quantities, supply responsibility, substrate information, programme, access rules, documentation requirements and the tender deadline to the [developers and main contractors page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/professionals/developers/).
