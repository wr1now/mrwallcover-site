# Wallcoverings for working hotels and busy corridors

Specifying wallcoverings for an occupied hotel: cleaning and durability, the sample room, batch control, phasing around guests, product documents, spares and handover.

By [Dorin Burcus](https://www.mrwallcover.com/about/#dorin), founder · Last updated 17 October 2026

Can wallcoverings be installed while the hotel stays open? Yes, and most of our hotel work has been in live buildings: [Brown’s Hotel](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/browns-hotel-mayfair/) never stopped welcoming guests across a two-year decorating programme, and [Raffles London at The OWO](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/raffles-london-the-owo/) and the hotel at [15 Old Bailey](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/old-bailey-hotel/) were fitted out room by room. The specification has to answer four questions that a residential job does not: how the wall will be cleaned, how the rooms will be released, how every room will match the sample room, and what the building needs to keep for maintenance afterwards.

## Which material suits a guest room, and which a corridor?

Corridors take luggage, trolleys and shoulders all day; guest rooms take chair backs and the occasional suitcase; reception and restaurant walls take hands and cleaning. [Contract vinyl](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/contract-vinyl/) is the usual specification where wear and cleaning come first, and at The OWO the install was a bespoke Vescom wallcovering to walls and parts of the joinery. Natural weaves and textiles still have a place where the finish matters more than wipeability: Phillip Jeffries Tweed and Omexco at [Four Seasons Ten Trinity Square](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/four-seasons-ten-trinity-square/), for instance. The decision is the room’s actual use, not the room’s name.

## What does the cleaning regime need?

Agree the housekeeping method before the product is chosen, then confirm the exact product’s cleaning instructions match it. Contract vinyl will take a damp cloth and mild soap; it will not take abrasive creams, neat bleach or scourers. A textile wall needs a dry method. Hand a care sheet per product to housekeeping at handover, because “wallpaper” is too broad a category for one cleaning method. Our [aftercare page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/aftercare/) gives the general care by family; the product’s own sheet takes precedence.

## Why insist on a sample room?

A sample room settles appearance, seam position, the junction with joinery and skirting, and the cleaning test, before the same work is repeated across a floor. At 15 Old Bailey the programme ran from a sample room in November 2022 to final touches in August 2024, covering 111 guest rooms across seven storeys. Record who accepts the sample room and on what basis, and keep that acceptance with the project record. Everything that follows is measured against it.

## How is batch and shading consistency kept across a floor?

Order each run of rooms from one batch, check the batch on every delivery against the measure, and allocate rolls to rooms before cutting so that no room mixes batches. With a bespoke production run, consistency from roll to roll and room to room is paramount; at The OWO, batches were checked and managed so that corridors of rooms read as one. Natural textiles vary more than vinyl, and shading on them was checked continuously at Four Seasons so the walls read as a single surface.

## How is the work phased around guests?

Identify which rooms can be released together, the permitted working hours, the delivery routes and where material can be stored. Delivery of free-issue goods is timed to the hang; paper is not left in corridors. The work is contained, moves around the house’s bookings, and the area is left tidy each day, with a project leader on site every day and, on a longer commission, a forward plan agreed each week. Include guest-facing protection, noise limits and the daily handover expectations in the scope, not as a courtesy afterwards.

## What documentation does the project need?

Where the project sets fire, acoustic or environmental requirements, obtain the current test or classification document for the exact product, backing and installation method proposed, and have it accepted before ordering. For fire, in building work, the designer, fire engineer or building-control body accepts it. Where the work is not building work, the hotel’s responsible person accepts it, through the fire risk assessment. Whoever set an acoustic or environmental requirement accepts that evidence. “Contract wallcovering” or “vinyl” on a sample card is not evidence of any classification, and neither is this guide. A classification belongs to the build-up that was tested, the wallcovering, adhesive and substrate together, so a substitute product or adhesive needs its own evidence. A guest room, a public corridor and a protected stair can carry different requirements; there is no single class that “hotels need”. Whether an older British classification satisfies a project is for the person who accepts it to decide, not the installer. The adhesive and primer are part of the specified system: for Vescom products, Vescom lists the correct ones per product on its [adhesives and accessories page](https://vescom.com/en/adhesives-accessories), and other makers’ instructions do the same for theirs.

## What should the hotel keep after handover?

A room-by-room record of the installed product, colourway, batch and care sheet; spare material from the same batch, kept dry with its batch label and stored as the maker recommends; and a clear note of the limits of matching a later repair, since a patch from a different batch will show. Our [problems and aftercare guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wallcovering-problems-and-aftercare/) explains what to record when something goes wrong.

## When does this advice stop being reliable?

It describes practice on London hotel refurbishments in occupied buildings. It does not certify any product. It does not replace the specifier’s review of fire, acoustic or wet-area requirements, or the responsible person’s fire risk assessment for a hotel in use. For a phased programme, send the room types, the floor or phase schedule, occupancy, working hours and the material codes; the [hotels and operators page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/professionals/hotels/) lists what to include, and the [developer package guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/developer-wallcovering-package/) covers the tender side where a main contractor is involved.

### Can you work nights or out of hours?

Out-of-hours work is on the published record. At Heathrow Terminal 4, a bespoke Calico Wallpaper mural was hung overnight on 24–25 September 2018 in a live terminal, and the finished walls were photographed between 02:30 and 03:42. In an occupied hotel, the permitted working hours, delivery routes, guest routes and daily handover are agreed for each project and written into the scope. Most of the hotel work on record was done in buildings that stayed open. Night shifts, out-of-hours and weekend work are offered, including in live hotels.

### Do you install Class A / B-s1,d0 fire-rated wallcoverings, and can you evidence it?

Mr Wallcover does not call a wallcovering fire-rated unless that product's own data says so. A fire classification, such as a Euroclass under EN 13501-1 (the standard's own class names, not a claim here), belongs to the build-up that was tested: the wallcovering, the adhesive and the substrate together. "Class A" on its own is not a class name under EN 13501-1: it may refer to a US ASTM E84 result, which is not a European class, or be loose shorthand for European class A1 or A2, so ask for the full classification, such as B-s1,d0. A substitute product or adhesive needs its own evidence. For a hotel, get the current classification document for the exact product, backing and installation method. For building work, the responsible designer, fire engineer or building-control body accepts it. Where the work is not building work, the hotel's responsible person accepts it, through the fire risk assessment. A guest room, a public corridor and a protected stair can have different requirements, and no single class is what "hotels need". The adhesive and primer are part of the specified system and are used as the maker lists them (Vescom, for example, lists them per product). The fire classification is not certified by the installer: it comes from testing and classification of the product in a stated build-up, and the wallcovering manufacturer declares it. Mr Wallcover installs to the wallcovering maker's instructions and the project's fire specification.

### How large a hotel programme can you run, and how long does it take?

These are the hotel programmes on record. Hyde London City: 111 rooms plus corridors and stair cores, from a finished sample room in November 2022 to final touches in August 2024, ahead of the opening on 2 September 2024. Raffles London at The OWO: bedrooms and several other areas, 2020–2023. Four Seasons Ten Trinity Square: guest rooms and suites, 2016–2019, through the 2017 opening. DoubleTree by Hilton London – Victoria: guest rooms, March to September 2023. Every project has a project leader on the ground, and the team that starts is the core team that finishes. The core team is six wallpaper hangers and six decorators, scaling to 25–35 people on large projects. At Hyde London City there were 25 people on site, in coordinated task teams covering preparation, filling, sanding, painting and hanging.

### How is snagging handled?

Snagging is part of the package. A sample room or sample area is agreed first, in the permanent lighting where possible, and every later room is measured against it. Then comes the hang, then snagging, then a return inspection about four to six weeks after completion, at no call-out charge, when seams, edges and corners are checked once the paper has settled. Visible seams and shading in natural weaves are properties of the material, and they are settled at the sample stage rather than raised as snags. At Hyde London City the record starts with a finished sample room in November 2022.
