# Is the wall ready for wallpaper?

How to tell whether a wall is ready for wallcovering: old coatings, dryness, soundness, filling and sanding, lining paper, priming and what needs a site inspection.

By [Dorin Burcus](https://www.mrwallcover.com/about/#dorin), founder · Last updated 18 October 2026

Is the wall ready? It is ready when it is sound, dry, smooth enough for the material, evenly porous and free of anything that will come away later. Most walls fail at least one of those on first inspection, and the expensive paper does not fix any of them. A fine finish makes an uneven wall more noticeable, especially under side light or with a smooth, reflective material such as [silk](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/silk-and-textiles/) or a plain contract vinyl.

## What does “sound” mean?

The surface has to hold. Old paint that is chalky or poorly bonded, filler sitting on dust, distemper, and the paper backing left from a previous hang will all let go under the pull of a drying wallcovering. The wall can look fine and still fail this test, which is why we look at the behaviour of the existing coating rather than its colour. A scratch test and a damp cloth tell you more than a photograph.

## Is it dry?

Fresh plaster, new filler and recent repairs need time and the right conditions before they are covered. A calendar date is not proof: a moisture reading is. Where there is any history of damp, a cold patch or a stain that has spread, the cause is investigated before anything decorative goes near it. Our [aftercare page](https://www.mrwallcover.com/aftercare/) explains why humidity and settling matter after the hang as well as before it.

## Is it smooth enough for this material?

Smooth enough depends on the covering. A textured grasscloth forgives small undulations that a silk or a plain vinyl will show in raking light. Where the specification includes it, preparation means stripping old paper, sanding with dust extraction, and making good cracks and uneven surfaces before anything decorative goes up. Period walls can take days of filling and sanding before they are true. At [Brown’s Hotel](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/browns-hotel-mayfair/), the hours spent filling and priming decided everything that followed on wide-width printed papers. An external edge that will not come true, such as an old arch, can be given a true line with a corner profile instead: our [imperfect arches case study](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/imperfect-arches-external-corner-trim/) shows how.

## What is lining paper for, and does this wall need it?

Lining paper is a plain paper hung before the finish. It does three jobs: it gives the finish one even, consistently absorbent surface instead of a patchwork of old paint, filler and plaster; it bridges hairline cracks and fine filler lines so they do not telegraph through; and it lets the joints of the finish sit away from anything beneath. It will not hold a coating that is letting go, cure damp, or straighten a wall that is out of true. Those are fixed first.

Whether it is needed is the maker’s call. [de Gournay](https://degournay.com/uploads/technical/document/2026_Hanging_Instructions-After-2025.pdf) says “all walls should be lined with plain white 1000 or 1200 grade” lining paper, hung with the same adhesive as the panels, and that cross-lining “is not necessary so long as the vertical joints between the lining panels do not meet up” with the joints of the finish. [Farrow & Ball](https://www.farrow-ball.com/how-to-guide/wallpaper/preparing-wallpaper) recommends horizontal cross-lining “around 1200–1400 grade”, left for at least 12 hours before hanging. [Zoffany](https://www.zoffany.design/faqs) recommends cross-lining too. Most contract vinyls are hung without a liner. Where lining is used, its joints never coincide with the joints of the finish, and it is dry before the top paper goes on.

## Why prime or size, and with what?

A primer or size controls suction so the adhesive dries at the right rate, and on vinyl and contract products it is what lets the covering be stripped cleanly years later. The right primer and adhesive are a pairing chosen for the wallcovering and the substrate together, and the product’s instruction names them. [de Gournay](https://degournay.com/uploads/technical/document/2026_Hanging_Instructions-After-2025.pdf) says new plaster and very absorbent surfaces “must be properly sized”, warns against oil-based primers that stay flexible, and says not to prime over its lining paper. [Farrow & Ball](https://www.farrow-ball.com/how-to-guide/wallpaper/preparing-wallpaper) asks for newly plastered walls to be “carefully sized”. [Zoffany](https://www.zoffany.design/faqs) does not recommend thinning its paste “unless you’re sizing a porous wall”. Phillip Jeffries asks for “a hard, acrylic, pigmented, wall-protecting primer” made for wallcovering installation under its grasscloths ([Grandview Grass instructions](https://api.phillipjeffries.com/files/instructions/Grandview%20Grass%20Hanging%20Instructions.pdf)). Vescom, whose bespoke wallcovering we installed at [Raffles London at The OWO](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/raffles-london-the-owo/), specifies its own Primer/Sealer, diluted or neat depending on the substrate, and its own adhesives on its [adhesives and accessories page](https://vescom.com/en/adhesives-accessories); that applies to Vescom wallcoverings, not to a residential paper or silk.

What none of them asks for is neat PVA brushed on as a universal sealer. It is the common shortcut, and it fails in a known way: it dries to a sealed, slick film that a water-based paste cannot soak into or grip, so the paper slides, dries slowly and can let go at the seams. Size or prime as the product’s instruction says. There is no single sealer or adhesive that solves every situation.

## Which finishes need the truest wall?

The ones that show the light. A smooth, reflective or very dark surface hides nothing. On the black matt Muraspec at the [Mercedes-AMG showroom](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/mercedes-amg-showroom-london/), the joints disappear only because the walls beneath were flat and the edges were cut clean: the preparation decided the result. A scenic paper is usually smooth and lit from the side, so the wall behind it has to be sound and true, and a textile wall lit from a window needs a truer substrate than one lit from the front. That is why the light in the room is part of the specification, and why the [designer checklist](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/designer-specification-checklist/) asks for it.

## Who chooses the paste, and how is it used?

The maker does. The adhesive is chosen with the primer for the wallcovering and the substrate together, and the product’s own hanging instruction names it; we use what it names rather than a house favourite. Whether a product is pasted on the wall or on the paper is set by the product too, and it changes the labour, which is why the [installation cost guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/installation-cost/) asks for a product code rather than a description. Where a lining is specified under a scenic paper, de Gournay asks for it to be hung with the same adhesive as the panels.

How the paste is handled matters as much as which one. Natural weaves and silks are hung with clean hands and an adhesive that suits the backing, and paste is kept off the face: on grasscloth it leaves a dark line, a velvet flock such as the one at [Pendley Manor](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/pendley-manor-hotel/) cannot be wiped clean of it, and a mark on black matt cannot be wiped away at all. A pasted scenic panel is never stretched to close a gap, because it shrinks back as it dries, and on a [digital mural](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/everyman-liverpool/) a drop stretched as it is pasted breaks the picture at the joint. On a hotel or any project with a fire requirement, the adhesive is part of the build-up that was tested, so a substitute needs its own evidence; the [hotel fire safety guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/hotel-fire-safety-and-compliance/) explains why.

## What needs a site inspection?

Photographs plan the job; they do not answer every question. Anything that cannot be confirmed until the old paper is off, a test area is tried or a moisture reading is taken should be recorded as uncertain in the scope, with an agreed way to authorise extra work. Typical cases: walls previously painted with gloss or a vinyl silk emulsion, walls with past water damage, new plaster that has not yet dried through, and walls where the specification calls for paper on joinery.

## How does preparation affect the programme?

Preparation discovered late moves the installation date, and that is expensive when deliveries, decorators, joinery and occupancy have already been arranged. Ask for preparation to be described separately in the quotation, with what is included and what is subject to inspection; our guide to [what changes the cost](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/installation-cost/) shows how. The survey is also a judgement on the wall: if it is not ready for the material specified, we say so before anyone orders the last drop.

## When does this advice stop being reliable?

For a quick reference, our [preparation tables](https://www.mrwallcover.com/wallpapering/preparation/) set out the treatment, primer and lining grade for each wall type and condition, and [lining papers, pastes and primers](https://www.mrwallcover.com/wallpapering/lining-and-adhesives/) lists the products with their data sheets. This is general guidance for domestic and hotel walls in reasonable condition. Damp, structural movement, lime plaster, historic fabric and any wall where the specification carries a fire or acoustic requirement need the product’s own instructions and, often, the specifier’s confirmation. The maker’s hanging instruction for the actual product takes precedence over everything on this page.

### Can you hang new wallpaper over old wallpaper?

Not reliably. Old paper, and the backing left after stripping, can let go under the pull of a drying wallcovering, and any joint or texture beneath shows through a fine finish. Where the specification includes preparation, the old covering is stripped and the wall made good first.

### Does wallpaper need lining paper?

Lining paper is a plain paper hung before the finish to give it an even, consistently absorbent surface: it evens out porosity, bridges hairline cracks and fine filler lines, and keeps the joints of the finish off anything beneath. It does not hold a failing coating, cure damp or straighten a wall that is out of true. Whether it is needed depends on the maker: de Gournay asks for all walls to be lined, Farrow & Ball recommends horizontal cross-lining, and most contract vinyls are hung without. The maker's instruction for the product comes first. Where the maker is silent, we follow our house standard: Erfurt lining paper and a lining paste at least one step above the wallpaper paste on our own scale, where the products' instructions permit the combination.

### Which wallpaper paste do you use?

The one the maker's instruction names for that product and substrate, together with its primer. Whether it is pasted on the wall or on the paper is set by the product. Where the project has a fire requirement, the adhesive is part of what was tested, so a substitute needs its own evidence.

### How do you know a wall is dry enough?

By a moisture reading, not by the calendar. New plaster, fresh filler and any wall with a history of damp are checked before they are covered, and the cause of any damp is investigated rather than hidden behind a new finish.
