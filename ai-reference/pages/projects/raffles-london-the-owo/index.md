## The brief

To install the hotel’s wallcoverings in the bedrooms and several other areas: a bespoke Vescom wallcovering created for the hotel, and Vescom Alcantara in some of the suites, applied to walls and, in places, to joinery. The rooms, designed by the late Thierry Despont, call for a calm, light-filled backdrop to Edwardian proportions.

## Our approach

**Scope, room by room.** Our method is to follow the survey with an estimate and a scope of works by room. Once agreed, that scope is the template for the job: openings come out of the measure, and pattern repeat and wastage go in.

**Preparation and priming.** Plain and finely textured wallcoverings forgive nothing. Every wall was prepared and primed to a true, even surface before installation, so that the light falling through tall Whitehall windows reveals texture, not substrate.

**Setting out.** Rooms were set out from their windows, doorcases and joinery, with seams planned to disappear in raking light. Where the wallcovering continued onto joinery, it was set out with the same discipline as the walls.

**Seam and shade control.** With a bespoke production run, consistency from roll to roll and room to room is paramount. Batches were checked and managed so that corridors of rooms read as one.

**Coordination.** Work was sequenced alongside the other trades and the main contractor during the building’s restoration.

## Outcome

We were chosen in 2021 to install about 17,500 m² of wallcoverings in 18 months, in the bedrooms and several other areas. Raffles notes that its rooms and suites feature customised carpets, fabrics and wallcoverings; it is a quiet satisfaction to have hung some of them.

## The building

Designed by William Young and completed in 1906 to the Edwardian Baroque, the Old War Office stands on Whitehall. After an eight-year restoration under the Hinduja Group and ONEX Holding it opened on 29 September 2023 as The OWO, home to Raffles London, the brand’s first hotel in the United Kingdom.

About the building

The building rose from 26,000 tons of Portland stone and some 25 million bricks. Young died before it was finished; his son Clyde Young and Sir John Taylor completed the design. It looks over the Royal Horse Guards and sits a short walk from Downing Street, Buckingham Palace and St James’s Park.

For almost six decades it was the seat of Britain’s military administration. Lord Haldane, Lord Kitchener, David Lloyd George and Winston Churchill all held office here as Secretary of State for War, and Douglas Haig served at the War Office as Director of Military Training and then Director of Staff Duties. In October 1914 a young T. E. Lawrence joined its Geographical Section. In 1960 John Profumo arrived as Secretary of State for War; his affair with Christine Keeler, and his resignation in 1963, nearly toppled the government.

Britain’s Secret Service Bureau, the forerunner of MI5 and MI6, was created in meetings at the War Office in 1909. Ian Fleming, a wartime naval intelligence officer, would have known the building, and its air of espionage helped inspire James Bond; the building has since appeared as MI6 in Bond films.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Each commission has a written scope by room, walls prepared until they are true, protected floors, batch and shade control, set-out from the room’s focal points, and a return visit. [Read the method](https://www.mrwallcover.com/practice/#method).

The six standards in full

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the pattern sits square to the cornice and the joinery.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
