## The collaboration

Estuary is Priya Ahluwalia’s first exploration of wallcoverings. Her watercolour paintings draw on the movement of water, migration, Indian and Nigerian architecture, and family photographs from the 1960s to the 1980s. Calico Wallpaper translated them into a large-scale mural. For the launch, the mural became the backdrop to Ahluwalia’s SS27 collection, *Revel*, on the runway at Rosewood London.

## The brief

Install the Estuary mural as the show backdrop within the production schedule, with the show the following day. The work had to be finished, clean and camera-ready in a working hotel event space that was being built around us.

## Our approach

- **Preparation first.** The backdrop surfaces were prepared and sanded before hanging, so that a mural this soft and luminous would read without a flaw under show lighting.
- **Panel sequencing.** A mural is a single image split across panels. Each one was hung in order and matched edge to edge so that the watercolour washes flow unbroken across the wall.
- **Working to the clock.** We worked alongside the show production crew and finished on the evening of 18 September, ready for the runway the next day.

## Outcome

On 19 September 2026 Ahluwalia showed *Revel* in front of Estuary, and the collection launched worldwide on Calico Wallpaper’s site the same week.

### The project

This was an event install for Calico Wallpaper, not a hotel fit-out. Calico hosted a dinner at NoMad London. The backdrop was a pair of temporary walls built off site. We papered the Estuary panels on those flats in the workshop and they were delivered finished. We did not hang them at the hotel.

### Our approach

- **Workshop installation:** papering on the flat, in controlled conditions, gives cleaner edges than hanging in a room that is about to host a dinner.
- **Edges and returns:** for freestanding panels the edges show from every angle, so the paper was wrapped and finished to look like a solid surface.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Whether the client is a hotel group, a designer or a household, the job runs the same way. These are the parts a photograph never shows.

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the design meets the cornice and the joinery with composure.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
