Three years of guest room and suite wallpapering, 2016 to 2019, through the opening of one of the City's great Beaux-Arts conversions, and the walls of the Four Seasons Private Residences upstairs.

## The building

Few addresses in London speak so eloquently of trade and empire. Designed by Sir Edwin Cooper, Ten Trinity Square was opened by the Prime Minister, David Lloyd George, in 1922 as the headquarters of the Port of London Authority. In 1946 it hosted a reception marking the inaugural meeting of the General Assembly of the United Nations, an occasion held in the building’s ballroom. The building also appeared in the 2012 James Bond film [*Skyfall*](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/finance/property/luxury-homes/11354656/Ten-Trinity-Square-a-home-in-one-of-Londons-greatest-buildings.html).

Acquired by Reignwood Group in 2010, the Grade II*-listed building underwent a seven-year restoration and conversion, led architecturally by Aukett Swanke, before opening as a Four Seasons hotel and private residences in 2017. It stands a short walk from the Tower of London and Tower Bridge, the neighbourhood whose name the hotel took in 2024.

## The brief

To paper the guest rooms and suites: premium finishes, among them Phillip Jeffries’ tailored Tweed, that had to sit comfortably beside restored heritage fabric, fine joinery and polished timber, and to read as one surface from the first room to the last.

## Our approach

**Survey.** Every space was surveyed before a single roll was opened, so that substrates, junctions and sight lines were understood rather than discovered.

**Preparation and priming.** Walls were prepared and primed to suit each material. With textile wallcoverings the substrate is everything; any flaw beneath a tweed or woven surface will eventually show.

**Setting out.** Drops were planned around windows, doors and joinery so that seams fell where the eye would not look for them, and so that each run began and ended with intent.

**Pattern and seam control.** Natural textiles vary from roll to roll. Batches, shading and seams were checked continuously so that walls read as one continuous surface.

**Coordination.** Across the years on site the team worked hand in hand with the electricians, the French polishers and the main contractor, sequencing our work around second-fix electrics and polished joinery so that no trade compromised another. The team’s organisation was as much a part of the finish as the craft itself.

## The private residences

The same building holds the Four Seasons Private Residences, and our work at Ten Trinity Square went beyond the hotel floors. The photographs at the top of this page are ours, taken in the residences in the spring of 2019.

**Entrance halls.** A long entrance wall hung in a dark, banded wallcovering, its streaks of silver and ochre running the full length of the hall without a visible seam, framed in pale panelling so it reads as one inset field. Banded patterns are unforgiving: every drop has to meet its neighbour on the line, or the bands step at the joins.

**Living and dining rooms.** Quiet, tailored walls behind walnut joinery, sculptural lighting and a brass-framed screen, the wallcovering finished cleanly into reveals and around fittings.

**Woven grasscloth.** A natural grasscloth turning an external corner, the weave kept straight and tight right to the edge, where a natural fibre is most likely to fray.

**Residents’ lift lobbies.** Warm, banded veneer- and stone-effect walls around the lift cores, matched from panel to panel so the figure flows around corners and past the lift doors.

Guest room and suite wallpapering from 2016 to 2019 on one of London’s most significant hotel conversions, through the opening in 2017 and the rooms that followed. Quiet, tailored walls that let Sir Edwin Cooper’s architecture take the applause.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Whether the client is a hotel group, a designer or a household, the job runs the same way. These are the parts a photograph never shows.

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the design meets the cornice and the joinery with composure.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
