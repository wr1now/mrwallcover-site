## The room

A guest bedroom with a long history, in a listed Georgian house. It is a room of character: a built-in writing desk, exposed beams and floorboards that slope with age. The owner is a private client, and in keeping with our policy we do not name them or the house.

## The scheme

House of Hackney’s design consultancy drew up the scheme. Rather than lighten the room, it leans into its dark, romantic character:

- **Walls:** BAMBUSA in Midnight, a painterly bamboo forest after dark
- **Bed hangings:** velvet around an antique four-poster, lined with House of Hackney’s SERPENTIS snakeskin
- **Ceiling:** SERPENTIS
- **Joinery:** fireplace, beams and bookshelves painted in the darkest blue
- **Finishing touches:** pineapple lamps and a brass [SERPENTIS candelabra](https://www.houseofhackney.com/products/house-of-hackney-serpentis-candelabra-brass)

## Our work

Our team hung the BAMBUSA wallpaper. A room like this is unforgiving to paper:

- **No straight lines.** In an old house, walls lean, floors slope and beams cut across the wall at odd angles. The bamboo stems are strong verticals, so each drop was set out with care to a laser level. The plumb line is the traditional method; the laser gives the same true vertical across the whole room at once, sloping floor and all. That way the forest reads as upright, even though the room around it is not.
- **Working around period joinery.** The paper is cut around the beams, the fireplace, the built-in desk and the shelving, then finished tight to the dark-blue paintwork.
- **Matching the drama.** Midnight is a deep ground. Joins have to be precise and edges clean, because any gap or lifted seam shows against the dark colour.

Our photographs from April 2019 show the room complete: BAMBUSA on every wall, the four-poster dressed, and SERPENTIS overhead.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Each commission has a written scope by room, walls prepared until they are true, protected floors, batch and shade control, set-out from the room’s focal points, and a return visit. [Read the method](https://www.mrwallcover.com/practice/#method).

The six standards in full

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the pattern sits square to the cornice and the joinery.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
