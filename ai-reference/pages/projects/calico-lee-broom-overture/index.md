## The collection

Overture grew out of Lee Broom’s childhood as a child actor and his fascination with theatre drapery: “the huge draped curtains with painted backdrops” and sets that played tricks with perspective. The wallpaper is a trompe-l’oeil curtain that turns a flat wall into a stage. First shown in New York, it was “reimagined for the London audience” in one of three rooms of Lee Broom’s Rivington Street showroom.

## Our approach

- **Measured for a bespoke layout.** We surveyed the room in July 2025 and supplied the dimensions from which Calico drew the panel layout. Overture is made to measure, so the folds of the curtain fall exactly where the room needs them.
- **Wall preparation.** The showroom walls were filled and sanded smooth. A drapery illusion only works if no real-world bump or ripple breaks the painted folds.
- **Large-format panels, double cut.** Calico’s fibre panels, about 68 cm wide, are hung with a bleed and double cut on the wall. That gives invisible seams through the soft gradients of the folds.
- **Hiding the door.** On the back wall the curtain runs straight across a flush door. The pattern is carried over it so that the door disappears into the drape.

## The dinner flats

For Calico’s London Design Festival dinner the same September, we also papered freestanding theatrical flats in a set builder’s workshop on 15 September 2025, ready to be moved into the dining venue.

## Outcome

From 16 to 21 September 2025, visitors to Shoreditch Design Triangle walked into a room that appeared to be draped in theatre curtains.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Each commission has a written scope by room, walls prepared until they are true, protected floors, batch and shade control, set-out from the room’s focal points, and a return visit. [Read the method](https://www.mrwallcover.com/practice/#method).

The six standards in full

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the pattern sits square to the cornice and the joinery.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
