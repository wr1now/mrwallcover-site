## The brief

To act as main decorating contractor across a sequence of rooms and suites: preparing every surface, hanging wallcoverings, fine painting, French polishing and specialist finishes, and spray-finishing the hotel’s richly moulded ceilings. All of it within a fully operating hotel, to programme, around guests in residence.

## Our approach

**Preparation and priming.** Period walls were filled, made good and primed until they were true enough to receive both fine paint and wide-width printed papers. On walls of this age, the hours spent here decide everything that follows.

**Setting out.** Adam’s Eden is a large-scale design with a 108.5 cm half-drop repeat. Each room was set out from its focal points (chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls) so that the birds and branches fall naturally, and so the pattern sits square to the cornice and the joinery.

**Ceilings.** The ornate moulded ceilings were spray-finished to give a flawless, even coat that keeps every crisp detail of the plasterwork, something a brush or roller cannot achieve on enrichment of this depth.

**Fine finishes.** Fine painting, French polishing and specialist finishes were carried out by the same team, so that joinery, walls and ceilings were finished as one coherent scheme.

**A live hotel.** Brown’s remained open throughout. We worked closely with the main contractor and the hotel’s management on access, timing, protection and housekeeping, keeping work contained and quiet. The project was delivered on time.

## The building

Brown’s opened in 1832, when James and Sarah Brown took 23 Dover Street. It has never been renamed, rebuilt or relocated. Rocco Forte Hotels acquired it in 2003.

About the building

The hotel was long said to date from 1837; its own commissioned research now places the opening in 1832.

Alexander Graham Bell made Britain’s first telephone call here in 1876. Rudyard Kipling came from the day he was married and returned for more than four decades; Agatha Christie’s *At Bertram’s Hotel* owes much to her stays. Theodore Roosevelt stayed before his marriage in 1886, and Franklin and Eleanor Roosevelt spent their honeymoon here.

How the commission ran

## The same standards, on every commission.

Each commission has a written scope by room, walls prepared until they are true, protected floors, batch and shade control, set-out from the room’s focal points, and a return visit. [Read the method](https://www.mrwallcover.com/practice/#method).

The six standards in full

01

### Survey and scope, room by room

We measure the wall that will actually be hung, allow for the repeat and the wastage, and write a scope of works room by room before anyone orders paper.

02

### Preparation decides the finish

Walls are filled, made good and primed until they are true. If a wall is not ready for the material specified, we say so before the last drop is ordered.

03

### Protection and containment

Floors, furniture and finished joinery are protected as part of the hang. Work is contained and the place is left tidy each evening. In a home we are visitors; in a hotel we work around the house.

04

### Batch and shade control

Deliveries are checked against the measure, and batches and shading are managed so that a corridor of rooms reads as one surface.

05

### Set out from the room's focal points

Pattern is set out from chimneypieces, doorcases and bed walls, so the pattern sits square to the cornice and the joinery.

06

### One team, then the return visit

The people who start are the people who finish. Four to six weeks after completion we come back to look at every seam and edge, and the workmanship is guaranteed for twelve months.
