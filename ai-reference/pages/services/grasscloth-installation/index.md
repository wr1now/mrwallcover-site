## Why the panels differ

Grasscloth is woven from natural fibres, such as sisal, raffia, arrowroot and jute, dyed and laid on a paper backing. Dye takes unevenly on natural fibre, so colour shifts along a roll and between rolls, and once hung each drop reads as a panel with its own tone. Light crossing the wall exaggerates the difference. That is the reason to look at a whole installed wall, not only a sample, before committing a room to it.

Printed and vinyl imitations remove the variation. If a uniform wall is what you want, compare one; the [choosing guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/choosing-wallcoverings/) covers that decision.

## What is normal, and what is a fault

Normal: a visible seam line, a step in tone between neighbouring drops, and a slightly different texture from one panel to the next.

Not normal, and never excused by shading: an open joint, a lifted seam, a dark line or dull patch from adhesive or water on the face, a shiny line where a seam roller has crushed the fibre, drops from two batches on one wall, a narrow sliver at the end of a wall that could have been balanced, or a crease, bubble or tear. Those are installation or handling problems, to be reported rather than lived with. The [grasscloth guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/grasscloth-seams-and-variation/) explains the difference in detail.

## Set-out and shading

Seams land where the wall widths, doors and windows put them, so on a prominent wall the set-out usually starts from the visual centre, the chimney breast or the bed wall, and the end panels are balanced so that neither is a sliver. The direction of the weave is agreed before the first length is cut. Our [set-out and pattern matching guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/set-out-and-pattern-matching/) explains how a room is set out.

The cut drops are shaded dry, one wall at a time; we hang three and stop to look. Whether alternate drops are reversed is decided by the product's own instruction and a trial on the wall, never by habit. The face is kept free of adhesive and smoothed with a plastic smoother, not a seam roller.

## One batch to a room

Variation between batches is larger than variation within one. The whole room is ordered, with a spare, from one batch number, and lengths from the same roll are kept together on the same wall where the room allows. Quantities allow for that, and for the fact that offcuts rarely blend; the [quantities guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/quantities/) explains why drops, not square metres, decide the order.

## Will it suit the room?

Natural grasscloth is a dry-dust surface. Moisture stains it, adhesive on the face marks it, and it is not suited to frequent wiping or splashes. In a busy hall or beside a kitchen, a textured vinyl may be the better answer if cleaning matters more than natural fibre.

The wall beneath needs to be sound, true and evenly porous, and lined where the maker specifies it. A survey decides that before anything is ordered; see [wall preparation](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wall-preparation/) and the [grasscloth and natural weaves](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/grasscloth-and-weaves/) material page.

### Will grasscloth seams be visible?

Yes. Visible seams and colour variation between rolls are part of the material. We agree how that will look, and the direction of the weave, before hanging.

### How do you clean grasscloth?

Gently, and dry. The care notes for each material are on the aftercare page.

### Who is responsible if free-issue material is faulty, short or damaged?

Free-issue deliveries are checked against the measure when they arrive. Scenic panels are checked dry against the layout before any paste and before anything is cut, so a sequence, sizing or print problem comes to light while it is still easy to resolve. Quantities are ordered to cover the repeat and the wastage, typically 15–30% and around 30% on staircases, so that the last wall doesn't wait. The specification should say who orders, who receives and checks the delivery, and who approves the batch. The twelve-month guarantee covers the workmanship of the hang: seams opening, edges lifting and adhesive failure that comes from the way the material was hung. Rolls are inspected as part of preparation, although on large volumes not every roll can be opened in advance. Any defect is reported straight away to the designer, the contractor and the manufacturer. If a fault comes from the installation, Mr Wallcover covers the cost of the material.

### How do you price, and what does it cost?

There is no flat rate per roll. The quotation comes from a site survey or from drawings. It reflects the measured area after openings are taken off, the pattern repeat and the wastage it creates (typically 15–30%, with staircases around 30%), the state of the walls, the material, and access and protection. Access, protection and making good are written into the scope rather than discovered at the end, and the quotation says what has been allowed for. The site publishes no prices.

### How is snagging handled?

Snagging is part of the package. A sample room or sample area is agreed first, in the permanent lighting where possible, and every later room is measured against it. Then comes the hang, then snagging, then a return visit about four to six weeks after completion, at no call-out charge, when seams, edges and corners are checked once the paper has settled. Visible seams and shading in natural weaves are properties of the material, and they are settled at the sample stage rather than raised as snags. At Hyde London City the record starts with a finished sample room in November 2022.

### What aftercare and guarantee are included?

Aftercare is part of every commission: a return visit about four to six weeks after completion, a priority repair call-out and a twelve-month workmanship guarantee.
