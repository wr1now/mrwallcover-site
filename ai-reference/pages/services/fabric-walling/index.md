Our approach

## Fabric on a wall is upholstery at room scale.

A fabric wall is quieter, warmer and softer than paper or paint, and it is the finish that most often shows the quality of the hands that put it up. The weave has to run true from corner to corner, joins have to fall where nobody looks, and every edge, socket and door frame has to finish crisply.

There are three ways to put fabric on a wall, and they are different trades. Paper-backed textiles are pasted and hung like a wallcovering. Traditional upholstered walling is stretched over battens and padding and can be buttoned. Modern seamless systems, most of them developed in France, hold the fabric in clip profiles around the edge of the wall. We have installed all three, and we help designers choose between them.

The options

## Three techniques, in detail

1. 01

### Upholstered walling with buttons

Battened, padded, buttoned

The traditional method. Timber battens are fixed around the perimeter of each wall and around doors, windows, sockets and switches. A padding or interlining, such as bump or wadding, is stretched across, then the fabric, with its widths sewn together where it needs to be wider than the roll, is stretched over and fixed to the battens. For a buttoned wall the button positions are set out on a grid with a laser, and each button is drawn through the padding and fixed so the fabric dimples evenly. The fixings are then hidden behind a braid, a gimp or a fabric-covered edge.

2. 02

### French seamless profile systems

Clip profiles, no adhesive

Slim PVC or aluminium profiles are fixed around the edge of the wall and around every opening. The fabric is cut oversize, tucked into the jaws of the profile with a spatula and tensioned at room temperature until it sits perfectly flat, then trimmed. Nothing is glued to the wall, wide fabrics can cover a wall without a seam, an acoustic fleece can go behind, and the fabric can be taken out and replaced later with the profiles left in place.

3. 03

### Paper-backed textiles

Hung like a wallcovering

Silks, linens, tweeds and other fabrics laminated to a paper or non-woven backing are pasted and hung like a wallcovering, on a lined, primed wall. This is how most textile wallcoverings in hotels are supplied and hung. See [silk wallcovering installation](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/silk-wallcovering-installation/) and the [silk and textiles](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/silk-and-textiles/) material page.

4. 04

### Fabric-wrapped panels

Made off the wall

Fabric stretched over boards or frames, made up and then fixed to the wall or to joinery as individual panels, like the fabric-wrapped panels in guest rooms at [Four Seasons Hotel London at Ten Trinity Square](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/four-seasons-ten-trinity-square/). For panels faced in wallcovering, see [joinery wallcoverings and panels](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/joinery-wallcoverings-and-panels/).

Buttons

## The buttoned wall, step by step

A buttoned wall is planned on paper first. The grid of buttons is drawn to the wall, with the spacing adjusted so that full diamonds or squares meet the corners, the skirting and the cornice evenly, and so that no button lands on a socket or a fixing.

Battens go up around the perimeter and every opening, and a backing is fixed where the buttons need something firm to pull against. The padding goes on next. The fabric is then stretched over, the buttons are drawn through and tied off, and the folds between them are dressed so they run in the same direction across the whole wall.

The grid is marked with a laser level. Across a wall several metres wide, a millimetre or two of drift per row shows clearly in a buttoned pattern.

French systems

## The French seamless profile systems

The clip-profile method was developed in France, where stretched fabric walls (tissu tendu) are a long-established finish. Three French makers are the names most often specified.

SWAL describes itself as the inventor of the clip profile for stretched ceilings and stretched fabric walls. Its ClipSWAL profiles are designed and made in France, in rigid PVC, and its SWALtradition wall fabric is tensioned cold, without heat, with an overall build-up of about 10 mm on the wall ([SWAL](https://www.swaldeco.com/fr/murs-tendus/tissu-tendu)).

CLIPSO, also French, makes its own profiles and wide-width textiles, including SO ACOUSTIC, a micro-perforated fabric used with a polyester fleece behind it for acoustic walls and ceilings ([CLIPSO](https://www.clipso.com/fr/realisations/prestige/acoustique-design-architectural-en-volume-3d.html)).

Barrisol, based in Kembs in Alsace and best known for stretched ceilings, makes Artolis, a range of technical wall textiles up to 5 m wide without a seam, installed at room temperature ([Artolis by Barrisol](https://artolis.eu/)).

We follow each maker's own instructions for the profile, the fabric and the fixings. Naming a maker records how a system works, not an endorsement or a supply arrangement.

Fire and acoustics

## Build-up, fire performance and sound

A stretched or upholstered wall is a build-up, not just a fabric. The fabric, the padding or fleece, the battens or profiles and the cavity behind are considered together, and in hotels and public buildings the fire classification of that whole build-up is checked against the specification before anything is ordered. Our [wallcovering fire ratings guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wallcovering-fire-ratings-uk/) explains why a rating for the fabric alone is not enough.

Padding and fleece also absorb sound. That is why fabric walls are chosen for meeting rooms, bedrooms and dining rooms, and why acoustic fabrics are made for the profile systems. For dedicated acoustic finishes, see [acoustic wallcoverings and panels](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/acoustic-wallcovering-installation/).

Process

## A fabric wall, step by step

1. 1

### Choose the technique

Upholstered, profile system or paper-backed: decided with the designer from the fabric, the room, the fire specification and whether the fabric may need to come down one day.

2. 2

### Survey and set-out drawing

Walls, openings, sockets and switches are measured, and the seams, pattern and any button grid are drawn so that they meet the corners evenly.

3. 3

### Prepare the wall

The wall is made sound and dry. Old coverings are taken off and the wall made good where needed.

4. 4

### Battens or profiles

Battens or clip profiles are fixed around the perimeter and every opening, set out with a laser so the lines are level and true.

5. 5

### Padding and fabric

Padding or fleece goes on, then the fabric is stretched, tensioned and fixed, with the weave kept straight across the whole wall.

6. 6

### Edges, trims and aftercare

Braid, gimp or profile edges are finished, socket and switch plates refitted, and the wall is checked again on the aftercare visit.

## Questions we are asked

Pause motion

### Can you hang wide-width fabric wallcoverings?

Yes. Wider goods need more hands and careful handling, especially at corners and openings. We plan for that in the scope.

### Do you install buttoned, upholstered fabric walls?

Yes. We fix battens, stretch padding and fabric over them, and set out and fix the buttons on a laser-marked grid, finishing the edges with braid, gimp or a fabric-covered edge.

### What is a seamless stretched fabric wall system?

Clip profiles, often from French makers such as SWAL, CLIPSO or Barrisol's Artolis range, are fixed around the edge of the wall. The fabric is tucked into the profiles and tensioned until it is flat, with no adhesive on the wall. Wide fabrics can cover a wall without a seam, and the fabric can be replaced later.

### Which is better, upholstered walling or a profile system?

It depends on the fabric and the look. Buttons and deep padding need the traditional method. A plain or patterned fabric that must run flat and unbroken suits a profile system. Paper-backed textiles are hung like a wallcovering. We advise once we have seen the fabric and the room.

### Who is responsible if free-issue material is faulty, short or damaged?

Free-issue deliveries are checked against the measure when they arrive. Scenic panels are checked dry against the layout before any paste and before anything is cut, so a sequence, sizing or print problem comes to light while it is still easy to resolve. Quantities are ordered to cover the repeat and the wastage, typically 15–30% and around 30% on staircases, so that the last wall doesn't wait. The specification should say who orders, who receives and checks the delivery, and who approves the batch. The twelve-month guarantee covers the workmanship of the hang: seams opening, edges lifting and adhesive failure that comes from the way the material was hung. Rolls are inspected as part of preparation, although on large volumes not every roll can be opened in advance. Any defect is reported straight away to the designer, the contractor and the manufacturer. If a fault comes from the installation, Mr Wallcover covers the cost of the material.

### How do you price, and what does it cost?

There is no flat rate per roll. The quotation comes from a site survey or from drawings. It reflects the measured area after openings are taken off, the pattern repeat and the wastage it creates (typically 15–30%, with staircases around 30%), the state of the walls, the material, and access and protection. Access, protection and making good are written into the scope rather than discovered at the end, and the quotation says what has been allowed for. The site publishes no prices.

### Do you take on single rooms, or only larger projects?

We focus on whole residences and hotel packages, and will take on one- or two-room commissions. The minimum installation commission is one room.

### What aftercare and guarantee are included?

Aftercare is part of every commission: a return visit about four to six weeks after completion, a priority repair call-out and a twelve-month workmanship guarantee.
