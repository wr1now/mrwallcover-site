Our approach

## Real materials behave like real materials.

A printed paper forgives a lot. Mica sheds at a careless cut, cork cracks if it is folded, metal leaf shows every fingerprint and every hollow in the wall, leather stretches, and veneer splits across the grain. These are the materials designers choose for the rooms that matter most, and they are the ones where installation decides the result.

We hang them as craft work: wall prepared and lined to a higher standard, the product checked roll by roll for batch, shade and damage before anything is cut, the layout drawn and agreed, seams set out with a laser, and the maker's instruction followed to the letter. Where a material will show natural variation or visible joins, we say so before the hang, not after.

The options

## The materials we install

1. 01

### Mica and metallic

Mineral sparkle, metal sheen

Mica wallcoverings are made from natural mineral flakes bonded to a paper backing, so they glint as the light moves. Metallic papers and foils have a reflective face. Both show every bump in the wall, both need fresh blades and clean edges, and metallic foils are kept away from live sockets and switches while they are trimmed.

2. 02

### Cork

Natural bark, warm texture

Thin cork veneer laminated to a paper backing, sometimes over a metal leaf ground so the gold or silver shows through, as in a Battersea bedroom we papered in 2019. Cork varies from roll to roll, cannot be creased, and its joins are part of its character. We also fix cork panels to walls. Phillip Jeffries cork wallcoverings are among those we have hung on several jobs.

3. 03

### Metal leaf

Gold, silver, copper

Squares of real or composition metal leaf laid by hand onto paper and sealed, so the grid of the leaves is part of the design. The face marks easily and the wall behind must be very flat. Phillip Jeffries makes many of these; we have hung leaf papers in Kensington and gilded wallcoverings in Pimlico.

4. 04

### Gilding

Gold on the detail

Gilding of cornices, mouldings, panel lines and joinery details with gold leaf or metal leaf over a size, sealed where the metal would otherwise tarnish. It is often planned in the same programme as a metal leaf or metallic wallcovering, so that the golds agree.

5. 05

### Leather panels

Hide on the wall

Real leather wall panels and tiles, flat or padded and stitched, fixed to a prepared wall or to a backing board. Hides vary in grain and colour, so panels are laid out and agreed before fixing. Padded leather walls are built up in the same way as [upholstered fabric walling](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/fabric-walling/). For leather-look finishes on joinery, see [architectural film wrapping](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/architectural-film-wrapping/).

6. 06

### Wood veneer

Real timber, flexible

Thin slices of real wood laminated to a paper or fleece backing. Veneer lengths are often numbered in the order they were cut so the grain flows across the wall, and they are hung in that sequence. Veneer is flexible along the grain and brittle across it, and in hotels and public buildings its fire classification is checked first.

Mica and metallic

## Mica and metallic papers

Mica is a natural mineral that splits into thin, glassy flakes. On a wallcovering the flakes are bonded to a paper backing, sometimes under a fine scrim, so they catch the light as you move through the room. Phillip Jeffries, for example, makes mica wallcoverings from authentic mica shavings ([Phillip Jeffries, Reflections](https://www.phillipjeffries.com/shop/CARD-AG-REFLECT)).

Mica sheds at a cut edge, so it is trimmed with a new blade every few cuts and the edges are handled as little as possible. Paste is kept off the face, because it dulls the flakes and cannot be washed out of them. In 2021 we installed Today Interiors Zaniah mica from a scaffold platform in a tall glazed atrium in Westminster, where daylight from above shows every seam.

Metallic papers and foils need the flattest wall of all. A reflective face turns every ridge of filler into a highlight, so the wall is filled, sanded with dust extraction and lined before it is hung, and checked in raking light at each stage.

Cork

## Cork wallcoverings and cork panels

Cork wallcoverings are a thin veneer of natural cork bark on a paper backing. Some are laid over a metal leaf or coloured ground, so the ground glints through the open grain of the cork. Others combine cork with wood veneer, like Phillip Jeffries' Cosmic Cork ([Phillip Jeffries](https://www.phillipjeffries.com/shop/CARD-NAT-COSMICCORK)).

Because it is bark, cork varies in colour and texture from roll to roll, and its seams are visible. We lay rolls out together before hanging to agree the order, and we never fold or crease a length, because cork cracks where it is bent sharply. Cork panels and tiles are set out from the centre of the wall with a laser so the cut pieces at the edges are even.

Metal leaf

## Metal leaf wallcoverings

Metal leaf wallcoverings are made by laying squares of gold, silver, copper or composition leaf by hand onto paper, then sealing them. Phillip Jeffries describes its Artistry Collection as handcrafted metallic leaf wallcoverings made in a multi-step process ([Phillip Jeffries, The Artistry Collection](https://www.phillipjeffries.com/shop/CARD-AG-ARTISTR)).

The overlapping edges of the leaves form a grid that is part of the design, so the grid is set out to run level and to meet the corners evenly. The face marks with fingerprints and creases at the slightest fold, so lengths are handled with clean gloves and supported along their whole length. Seams are visible by nature, and we agree how they will look before the hang.

Gilding

## Gilding and gold detail

Gilding puts real metal on the architecture itself: the run of a cornice, the bead of a panel moulding, the edge of a door or a carved detail. A size is applied to a sealed surface, the leaf is laid onto it once the size reaches the right tack, the loose leaf is brushed away, and the gilding is sealed where the metal would otherwise tarnish.

We offer gilding on its own or in the same programme as a metallic or metal leaf wallcovering, so that the gold on the walls and the gold on the detail are chosen and sampled together.

Leather

## Leather panels and leather walls

Leather walls are made from real hides, either as flat tiles and panels fixed to a prepared wall or backing board, or padded, stitched and upholstered in the same way as a fabric wall. Hides vary in grain and shade, so every panel is laid out, numbered and agreed before it is fixed.

On the record we have hung a Roberto Cavalli Home leather-effect wallcovering on the walls and ceilings of a private apartment in Southwark. That is a wallcovering, not real leather, and we describe it as such.

Wood veneer

## Wood veneer wallcoverings

Wood veneer wallcoverings are thin slices of real timber laminated to a flexible backing, so a wall can be lined in walnut, oak or a figured veneer without solid panelling. Phillip Jeffries groups its wood veneer and cork wallcoverings together ([Phillip Jeffries, Wood Veneers](https://www.phillipjeffries.com/shop/categories/WOOD+VENEERS)).

Veneer lengths are often numbered in the order they were cut, so the grain flows across the room; they are hung in sequence, never shuffled. Veneer bends along the grain but splits across it, so it is never creased, and the wall behind is made flat and primed exactly as the maker asks. In hotels and public buildings, the fire classification of the veneer and its build-up is checked against the specification first; see our [fire ratings guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wallcovering-fire-ratings-uk/).

Process

## A specialist wallcovering, step by step

1. 1

### Specification and samples

We read the maker's instruction, check the fire classification where it matters, and look at a sample in the room's own light with the designer.

2. 2

### Check every roll

Batch, shade, sequence numbers and any damage are checked on delivery, and rolls are laid out together to agree the order before anything is cut.

3. 3

### Prepare and line

The wall is made good, sanded with dust extraction, lined and primed to the standard a reflective or natural material needs, then checked in raking light.

4. 4

### Set out with a laser

Seams, leaf grids, panel joints and veneer sequences are set out with a laser so they run level and meet the corners evenly.

5. 5

### Hang or fix

Each length or panel is cut with fresh blades, pasted or fixed exactly as the maker asks, and handled with clean gloves to keep the face unmarked.

6. 6

### Aftercare

A return visit once the room has settled, to check seams, edges and corners, and the twelve-month workmanship guarantee.

### Do you install mica wallpaper?

Yes. We have installed mica and mica-effect wallcoverings in London, including Today Interiors Zaniah in a glazed atrium in Westminster. Mica is trimmed with fresh blades, paste is kept off the face, and the wall is prepared and lined first.

### Can you hang cork wallpaper and cork panels?

Yes, both. Cork wallcoverings, including cork over a metal leaf ground and Phillip Jeffries cork papers, and cork panels set out with a laser from the centre of the wall.

### Are the seams visible on metal leaf wallcoverings?

Usually, yes. Hand-laid leaf forms a visible grid and the joins between lengths are part of the material. We set the grid out to run level and agree how the seams will look with you before the hang.

### Do you do gilding?

Yes. We gild cornices, mouldings, panel lines and joinery details with gold leaf or metal leaf, on its own or alongside a metallic or leaf wallcovering.

### Do you install wood veneer and leather wall panels?

Yes. Wood veneer wallcoverings are hung in their cut sequence so the grain flows, and leather panels are laid out and agreed before fixing. In hotels and public buildings we check the fire classification of the build-up first.
