Our approach

## Removal decides how good the next wall can be.

Taking wallpaper down looks like the easy part of a decorating job. It is the part that most often goes wrong. Too much water blows old plaster. A scoring tool pressed too hard cuts the face paper of plasterboard, and the cuts show through the next finish in side light. Adhesive left on the wall stops paint and paste from bonding.

So we treat removal as preparation, not demolition. We find out what the covering is and how it was hung, test a small area, choose the gentlest method that works, and plan the making good from the start. The result should be a wall that is sound, clean, flat and ready for whatever goes on it next.

The options

## Wallcoverings and how each comes off

1. 01

### Vinyl wallcoverings

Two separate steps

Many vinyl wallcoverings, including the white and plain contract vinyls common in hotels, offices and rented flats, have a plastic face that can be stripped off dry, often in whole lengths. That is only the first step. The paper or fabric backing and the adhesive stay on the wall, and they are removed as a second, separate step by soaking and scraping, then washing the wall down until no paste residue is left.

2. 02

### Paper wallpapers and lining paper

Two layers, not one

Traditional papers are usually wet-removable: soaked or steamed until the paste softens, then lifted with a broad scraper. Many were hung over a lining paper, and in most cases the lining comes off as well. A new wallcovering should not sit on an old lining whose bond nobody can vouch for, because the pull of a drying paper can lift it. Our [wall preparation guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wall-preparation/) explains why.

3. 03

### Dry-strippable non-wovens

The quickest, when it works

Papers sold as dry strippable are made to come away whole and dry, without soaking, and many modern non-wovens behave this way. Peelable papers are different: the top layer comes off dry and the backing stays on the wall. We check which one we are dealing with before deciding the method, because a label on a roll that left years ago is rarely to hand.

4. 04

### Painted-over wallpaper

The slowest

Paint seals the paper and stops water getting to the paste, so painted-over paper is perforated with care, soaked or steamed in sections, and scraped. It is slow, and the wall underneath is often in worse condition than it looked, so we allow for extra making good.

5. 05

### Specialist and fabric wallcoverings

Silk, grasscloth, fabric

Silks, grasscloths, paper-backed fabrics and stretched [fabric walling](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/fabric-walling/) each come off in their own way. Fabric on battens is unpicked rather than soaked, and the battens and staples are taken out and the holes filled. Joinery, cornices and adjoining finishes are protected throughout.

6. 06

### Ceilings and stairwells

Height and access

Papered ceilings and tall stairwells are stripped from mobile towers or platforms, with the same testing and protection as the walls. Lining on old ceilings is checked carefully, because lath-and-plaster ceilings must not be soaked heavily.

Vinyl

## Why vinyl removal needs more repair

Contract vinyls are hung with strong, heavy-duty adhesives. Where the wall was not primed or sealed before they went up, that adhesive soaks into the plaster or the plasterboard paper and becomes part of the surface. When the backing is scraped off, the adhesive can take the surface with it.

It can also pull out the filler used in previous making good. Old cracks, screw holes and patched joints reappear, and filled areas can come away in pieces. So with white vinyl wallcoverings we always allow for more repair than with a traditional paper: re-filling, spot-sanding and, where the plasterboard face is torn, a penetrating sealer such as Zinsser Gardz before any new filler or finish.

This is also why we prime properly when we hang a vinyl ourselves. Our [wall preparation guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/advice/wall-preparation/) notes that on vinyl and contract products, the primer is what lets the covering be stripped cleanly years later.

The substrate

## Plaster, plasterboard and what is underneath

Solid plaster on older brick walls usually tolerates soaking and steam, though very old lime and gypsum plasters soften if soaked for too long, and hollow or loose areas show up once the paper is off. We tap the wall, mark anything hollow, and decide with you whether to fill, patch or replaster.

Plasterboard is less forgiving. Its face is paper, so heavy steam or soaking softens it, and a scraper held too steeply tears it. On unskimmed board we keep water to a minimum, work in short passes, and seal any torn face before filling.

Some older walls and ceilings carry a textured coating under the paper. In buildings from before 2000 these coatings can contain asbestos, and the [Health and Safety Executive](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/guidance/a28.pdf) treats steaming or scraping them as asbestos work. If we find one, we stop and ask for it to be tested before anything else happens.

Making good

## After the strip: making good, sizing and priming

In most cases some making good is needed once the paper is off: cracks opened up, nail and screw holes, torn board paper, lifted filler and the odd hollow patch. We fill, leave it to dry, and sand with dust extraction, using dustless sanding stations, giraffe sanders and Festool and Mirka sanders on extractors.

Then the wall is washed down, checked in raking light, and sealed. If the next finish is paint, it is primed for paint. If it is a new wallcovering, it is sized or primed with what the maker of that covering asks for, and lined where the specification calls for it. Our [lining papers, pastes and primers](https://www.mrwallcover.com/wallpapering/lining-and-adhesives/) page lists what we use and why.

A new paper is set out with a laser level, so the first drop goes up true on a wall that has just been made flat.

Protection

## Clean, contained and out of hours

Stripping is wet and messy. Floors are covered and taped, furniture is moved or wrapped, sockets and switches are isolated and protected, and old paper goes straight into bags rather than across the floor.

In occupied homes and hotels we close the work area with ZipWall partitions and run HEPA air filters, so dust from sanding stays where it is made. In hotels we work a few rooms at a time, and overnight shifts and weekends are available where the building cannot close.

Process

## Wallpaper removal, step by step

1. 1

### Survey and test patch

We identify the covering, look for lining, paint or textured coatings beneath, tap for hollow plaster, and strip a small test area to see how the wall behaves.

2. 2

### Protect the room

Floors, joinery, furniture and electrics are protected. In occupied buildings the area is closed with ZipWall partitions and HEPA air filters are switched on.

3. 3

### Remove the face

Vinyl faces and dry-strippable papers are lifted dry. Painted or paper faces are perforated with care where needed, never deep enough to mark the wall.

4. 4

### Soak or steam, then scrape

Backing paper, lining and paste are softened with water or steam in controlled sections and lifted with a broad scraper held low, then the wall is washed free of adhesive.

5. 5

### Make good

Cracks, holes, torn board paper and lifted filler are sealed, filled and sanded with dust extraction, then the wall is checked in raking light.

6. 6

### Seal, size or prime

The wall is left primed for paint, or sized, primed and lined for a new wallcovering, ready to be set out with a laser.

## Questions we are asked

Illustrative material studies

Pause motion

### Can wallpaper be removed without damaging the wall?

Usually the wall survives well, but not always untouched. Old plaster can be hollow under the paper, plasterboard face paper can tear, and adhesive can pull out old filler. We test first, use the gentlest method that works, and plan the making good as part of the job rather than as a surprise.

### Why does removing vinyl wallpaper need more repairs?

The vinyl face often strips off dry, but the backing and adhesive stay behind and come off as a second step. Contract adhesives are strong, and on a wall that was not primed they soak into the surface, so scraping them off can lift the plaster skin, the plasterboard paper or filler from earlier repairs. We allow for more making good on vinyl than on a traditional paper.

### Do you remove the lining paper as well?

In most cases, yes. Paper wallpapers are often hung over a lining, and an old lining whose bond cannot be checked is not a safe base for a new wallcovering. Where the specification is a new lining, the wall is made good and lined again.

### How much does wallpaper removal cost?

There is no fixed price per room, because the cost depends on what is on the wall: the type of covering, how many layers, whether there is lining or paint over it, the area and height, the condition of the plaster or board underneath, how much making good is needed, the protection required and whether the work is out of hours. We quote from a survey, or from photographs and measurements. The [installation cost guide](/advice/installation-cost/) explains each item.

### Can you strip wallpaper while we live in the house, or while the hotel is open?

Yes. We work a room or a few rooms at a time, close the area with ZipWall partitions, run HEPA air filters and sand with extraction. In hotels we can work overnight or at weekends so that guests are not disturbed.

### Do you use a steam stripper?

Where the wall can take it. Steam works well on paper over sound plaster. On plasterboard and on fragile old plaster we use as little moisture as possible, in short controlled passes, because too much softens the surface.

### Can you hang the new wallpaper afterwards?

Yes, and that is most of our work. Removal, making good, sizing or priming, lining and the new hang are planned as one programme, with the new paper set out with a laser level. If the room is going back to paint, we leave the wall primed and ready.
