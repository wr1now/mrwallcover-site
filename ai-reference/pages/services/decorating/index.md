Our approach

## Preparation decides the finish.

In a prime house or a five-star bedroom, there is nowhere for a poor finish to hide. Raking light along a corridor shows every ridge in the filler, and a dark lacquered door shows every brush mark. So most of our time goes on the work you never see: protection, repairs, filling, sanding, lining and priming.

We are wallcovering installers as well as decorators. That matters, because the paint and the paper are planned as one programme, in the right order, by one team, with one person answerable for the result.

The options

## Finishes and services, in detail

1. 01

### Walls and ceilings

Flat, even, durable

Repairs, filling and lining where the plaster needs it, then a primer and finish coats in the sheen the room calls for: flat matt for period rooms, harder-wearing finishes for corridors and family rooms.

2. 02

### Joinery, doors and skirtings

The close-up work

Doors, architraves, skirtings, panelling, shutters and built-in joinery, sanded and finished by hand. Deep colours on joinery, like the navy doors at Brown's Hotel, need the most careful preparation of all.

3. 03

### Cornices and mouldings

Period detail

Ornate plaster cornices, ceiling roses and mouldings cleaned back and painted without filling the detail. Damaged runs are flagged at the survey, before any paint goes on.

4. 04

### Limewash

Soft, mineral walls

Applied by our own team in layers for walls with depth and movement. Each wall is sampled first, because limewash changes with the surface and the light.

5. 05

### Feature colour and lines

Bands, dados, stripes

Colour blocks, painted bands and decorative lines set out with a laser so they stay level and true around a whole room. The plumb line is the traditional method; it is not how we set out.

6. 06

### Paint and paper together

One programme

Ceilings, joinery and plain walls painted around wallcoverings of every kind, so edges, colours and cut lines are planned rather than patched afterwards. [Window film](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/window-film/) can go in the same programme.

7. 07

### Exteriors and render

The outside too

Scaffolded façades as one commission with the interior, as at St George's Square and Inverness Terrace: see [exterior works](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/exterior-works/). Lime render is arranged through trusted specialist partners and programmed with our work.

Preparation

## What preparation actually includes

Protection of floors, furniture, art and anything already finished; dust control and a daily clean-down; filling and sanding; repairs to cracks and joints; lining where the plaster needs it; spot-priming and full priming; and checks in raking light before each finish coat.

Older houses can hide old lead-based paint and lime plaster. Both are flagged at the survey, and the method is chosen to suit them rather than forcing a modern system onto a breathable wall.

Equipment

## The kit behind a clean, fine finish

Fine spray finishes: three Graco HVLP spray stations for joinery, doors and furniture-grade surfaces, and two Graco airless sprayers for walls and ceilings on larger runs. Spraying gives an even, brush-mark-free coat where a room calls for it.

Dust-controlled sanding: three giraffe long-reach sanders for walls and ceilings, three dustless sanding stations, three Festool hand sanders and Mirka sanders, all extracted at the pad. Far less dust reaches the room, which is what makes sanding workable in occupied homes and in hotels that stay open.

Contained work areas: ZipWall systems put up temporary dust partitions floor to ceiling, so the work area is limited to the rooms being decorated and the rest of the house or hotel carries on.

Cleaner air: air-filtering stations with HEPA filters run while we prepare and paint, so the air stays cleaner for the people living or staying around the work.

Process

## The job, step by step

1. 1

### Survey and schedule

We walk the rooms, note the surfaces and repairs, and agree a written schedule of what is painted, in which finish, and in what order.

2. 2

### Sample in the room

The colour, sheen and finish are sampled on the wall so they can be approved in the room's own light before the full run starts.

3. 3

### Protection

Floors, furniture, joinery and anything already finished are protected, and in hotels the work area is kept separate from guests.

4. 4

### Preparation

Cracks, joints and old paint are dealt with properly: filling, sanding, lining and priming. This is where the finish is decided.

5. 5

### Set-out and finish coats

Lines, bands and wallcovering positions are set out with a laser, then finish coats are applied and checked in raking light.

6. 6

### Snagging and aftercare

A snagging walk with you before handover, then the same aftercare as our wallcovering work if anything needs attention.

## Questions we are asked

Illustrative material studies

Pause motion

### Can you decorate while we live in the house or the hotel stays open?

Yes. Rooms are protected and worked in an agreed order, and in hotels we work room by room and out of hours where needed, so the building keeps running.

### Do you apply limewash yourselves?

Yes. Limewash is applied by our own team. Lime render is arranged through trusted specialist partners.

### Can we see the finish before you start?

Yes. A sample of the colour and finish is applied in the room so it can be approved in its own light before the full run.

### What should I send for a decorating quotation?

The address or area, the rooms involved, drawings or a schedule if you have them, the finishes in mind and any dates the building has to work around. Every decorating job is quoted after a survey.

### How much does painting and decorating cost in London?

It depends on the condition of the surfaces, the amount of preparation, the finishes, access and whether the work is out of hours. We quote after a survey, against a written schedule, so you can see exactly what is included.

### How long does it take to decorate a room?

A straightforward room in good condition can take a few days; a period room with cornices, joinery and repairs takes longer, because preparation and drying times cannot be rushed. We give you a programme with the quotation.

### Do you decorate listed and period buildings?

Yes. Hyde London City is Grade II listed, and much of our residential work is in period houses. Where a listed building needs specific materials, such as limewash, we follow the specification and the conservation officer's requirements.

### Do you work with interior designers and main contractors?

Yes. We work to the designer's specification and the contractor's programme, give samples for sign-off and report progress room by room.

### Do you supply the paint or can we choose it?

Tell us at the survey. Where a designer or specification names the paint, we work to it; otherwise we advise on the right product and sheen for each surface.

### Do you hang wallpaper as well as paint?

Yes. Wallcovering installation is our core trade, so a room can be painted and papered by one team in one programme.

### How do you protect floors, furniture and art?

Everything is covered or moved under an agreed plan before work starts, dust is controlled, and the room is cleaned down at the end of each day.

### Do you produce sample boards or sample panels first?

Yes. The colour and finish are sampled in the room itself, so they are approved in that room's light before the full run starts.
