Our approach

## One survey, the right film, a clean edge.

Most window film companies sell film. We start with the room. A south-facing drawing room losing its silk to the sun, a hotel bedroom above a night bus route and a ground-floor office that people can see straight into are three different problems, and each needs a different film.

Because we are decorators and wallcovering installers first, we see the damage that sunlight and noise do to finished interiors. Film is often the cheapest way to protect a scheme that has just been hung, painted or upholstered.

The options

## Seven kinds of film, and what each one is for

1. 01

### Solar control

Heat and glare

Reflects or absorbs part of the sun's energy, so south- and west-facing rooms stay cooler, blinds can stay up and screens stay readable. Neutral and low-reflective grades keep the look of a period façade.

2. 02

### UV protection

Fading

Clear films that filter most of the ultraviolet behind faded fabrics, timber floors, artwork and wallcoverings. The view and the daylight stay almost unchanged, so it suits listed and prime homes.

3. 03

### Acoustic

Street noise

Laminating films add damping to a single pane and take the edge off traffic and voices. They improve the glass you have. Where noise is severe we will tell you that secondary glazing is the bigger step.

4. 04

### Privacy

The view in

Frosted, gradient and one-way-by-day films screen bathrooms, meeting rooms and street-level windows without losing light. Daytime one-way films reverse at night when the room is lit, and we explain that before you choose.

5. 05

### Safety and security

Broken glass

Clear films that help hold broken glass together, for low-level glazing, doors and places where a break would be dangerous. Thicker security grades slow down a forced entry.

6. 06

### Decorative and manifestation

Pattern and identity

Etched-look bands, patterns, logos and manifestation strips on full-height glass, cut to the pane and set out by laser so the lines run true across a whole run of doors.

7. 07

### Anti-graffiti

Scratches and tags

A clear sacrificial layer on shopfronts, lift glass and mirrors. When it is scratched or tagged, the film is replaced and the glass underneath is untouched.

Plain English

## Glass checks, safety rules and listed buildings

Not every film suits every pane. Darker or reflective films can heat the glass, which matters on some sealed double-glazed units and older glass, so the film maker's guidance for that glazing type decides what we fit.

Building Regulations ask for some low-level and full-height glass to be safe if it breaks, and for large areas of glass to be clearly marked. Safety film and manifestation film are common ways to deal with existing glass; your designer, building manager or building control confirms what each pane needs.

On listed buildings, internal film is usually reversible and leaves the glass untouched, but check with the conservation officer first. We supply the product details for that conversation.

### Survey

We look at the glass type, the frames, the orientation and what the room needs. The film maker's guidance decides what can go on that glazing, especially sealed double-glazed units.

One clear recommendation, not a catalogue. On larger jobs a sample pane is fitted first so the look and the light can be approved in the room.

Decorative bands and manifestation lines are set out with a laser so they run level across every pane. The plumb line is the traditional method; it is not how we set out.

4. 4

### Preparation

Furniture and floors are protected, and the glass is cleaned to a high standard. Most problems people see in film, from specks to lifted edges, start with poor cleaning.

5. 5

### Installation

Film is cut to the visible pane, squeegeed out and finished neatly to the frame. In hotels and offices we work room by room or floor by floor, out of hours where needed.

### Curing and aftercare

A slight haze while the adhesive cures is normal. We leave cleaning instructions on the day and come back if anything needs attention.

### What should I send for a window film quotation?

Tell us where the glazing is and whether you need decorative, privacy, solar control or safety film. The glazing type, frames and orientation need to be checked against the film maker's guidance before a product is recommended. Include whether you need film supplied as well as installed.

### Which type of window film do I need?

Decorative or frosted film for screening and design, privacy film to hide a view, solar control film for heat and glare, and safety or security film to help hold broken glass together. Some films combine more than one job. We recommend one after seeing the glass and the room.

### Can window film go on double glazing?

Often, but not every film suits every unit. Solar films in particular can raise the temperature of the glass. We check the glazing type and follow the film maker's guidance on what can be applied.

### Can window film be removed later?

Yes. Film can be taken off and the glass cleaned, which makes it useful for rented premises, changing branding or a room that changes use.

### Does window film soundproof a window?

It reduces noise rather than removing it. Acoustic laminating films damp the glass and soften traffic and street noise. For heavy noise, secondary glazing does more, and we tell you that at the survey rather than oversell the film.

### Will UV film stop wallpaper and furniture fading?

It removes most of the ultraviolet that drives fading, which slows it a great deal. Visible light and heat also play a part, so a solar grade can be combined where a room gets strong sun.

### Can you film a hotel or office that stays open?

Yes. At Hyde London City every window was filmed as part of the room-by-room decorating package, and at Gray's Inn Square offices were done floor by floor. We plan the order with the building team and can work out of hours.

### How much does window film cost in London?

It depends on the film, the size and number of panes, access and whether the work has to be done out of hours. We quote after a survey or from photos and measurements, and the quotation names the film so you can compare like with like.

### How long does window film last?

Internal film from a recognised maker lasts many years. The maker's warranty for that film and that glazing is the real answer, and we give it to you with the quotation.

### Will window film affect my double glazing warranty?

It can if the wrong film goes on the wrong unit. We check the glazing type against the film maker's guidance before recommending anything,.

### Can people see in at night through one-way privacy film?

Yes, when the room is lit and it is dark outside. One-way films work by day. For privacy at night we recommend frosted or gradient film, or film combined with blinds.

### Can window film go on a listed building?

Internal film is usually removable and does not change the glass, which is why it is often used on listed buildings, as at Hyde London City. Always check with your conservation officer or building manager first; we can supply the product details.

### Will I notice the film once it is on?

Clear UV, safety and acoustic films are close to invisible. Solar films can tint or slightly reflect the glass, so we show a sample before the full job.

### Will window film affect phone signal?

Some metallised solar films can weaken mobile and Wi-Fi signals. Where signal matters, a non-metallic film is chosen, and we raise it at the survey.

### How do I clean window film, and how long does it take to cure?

Leave it alone while the adhesive cures; a slight haze or small water pockets can take a short while to clear, depending on the film and the weather. After that, clean with a soft cloth and a mild, non-abrasive cleaner. We leave the maker's care instructions on the day.

### How long does installation take, and can you work at night in a hotel?

Most rooms take hours, not days. In hotels we work room by room, out of hours where needed, so rooms go back on sale the same day, as we did across every window at Hyde London City.
