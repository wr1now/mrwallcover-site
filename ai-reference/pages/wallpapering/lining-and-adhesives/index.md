# Lining papers, pastes and primers

The products we use when the wallpaper maker is silent, the paste ladder behind our one-step-stronger rule, and what each maker asks for. Product coverage is quoted from the maker’s data sheet or page with the date checked. Strength steps are our house scale, not a rating shared by manufacturers.

No maker pays to appear here, and there are no affiliate links.

## Our paste scale and lining practice

Mr Wallcover uses the four steps below to describe our own paste choices. Where the product’s instructions permit it, our house practice is to choose a lining paste at least one step above the wallpaper paste. These steps do not establish compatibility between different brands or substrates. If either paste’s position is unknown, or there is no higher step, we check the complete combination before ordering. The wallpaper and adhesive makers’ current instructions take precedence.

2. MediumStep 2
3. HeavyStep 3

## Woven wallcoverings: grasscloth, silk and textiles

Natural and woven faces are hung with the paste their maker names, and the paste never touches the face: on grasscloth it leaves a dark line, and on silk it cannot be wiped away. Cole & Son asks for its ready-mixed starch paste on its silks and grasscloths and warns against heavy-duty pastes; Fromental asks for a good-quality pre-mixed wallcovering adhesive on its grasscloth.

Where the maker gives no paste, we hang grasscloth, fabric, hessian and linen with a Medium-grade paste. The medium grades we use are made for them: Beeline's Medium Grade is formulated for hanging a wide range of Wallcoverings including textiles, glass fibre, hessian, paper and fabric-backed contract vinyl Wallcoverings, and Albany's Speciality, a medium grade, lists hessians, grasscloth, suedes, flocks, textiles. Our usual lining paste is Extra-heavy on the house scale. We confirm that it suits the particular wallpaper, lining, substrate and both makers’ instructions before using that combination.
