## Material decisions before the first length is cut

Choose by the exact product, the room and the intended finish. Physical samples, the maker’s instructions and a sound substrate matter more than the family name.

### Paper and non-woven

You want a printed pattern or a quieter, more even visual finish.

**The trade-off:** Print, backing and care vary substantially between products.

**Before ordering:** Confirm the repeat, match, usable width and actual cleaning instructions.

Appearance, joins, handling & care

**Setting**
: Homes and hotels

**Appearance**
: Printed or plain, from a quiet texture to a large repeat.

**Joins**
: Seams are part of a hung wall. The match follows the repeat: free, straight or a half-drop. Half-drop papers and murals are set out on site; the calculator gives the order quantity, not the set-out.

**Handling**
: The wall has to be sound first. Lining is used when the maker or the wall requires it.

**Care**
: Follow the maker. Many papers are not washable. We do not call a family washable, fire-rated or seamless unless that product’s own data says so.

[Read the material guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/paper-and-non-woven/) · [Installation service](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/#installation)

### Grasscloth and natural weaves

Natural fibre and a visible panel character are part of the look you want.

**The trade-off:** Adjacent drops can differ in shade; do not expect an invisible joint.

**Before ordering:** Review a full-wall example, panel layout and the exact care sheet.

Appearance, joins, handling & care

**Setting**
: Rooms where variation is wanted

**Appearance**
: Sisal, raffia, grass and other weaves. Colour and texture move from roll to roll.

**Joins**
: Seams show. The direction of the weave is agreed before the first length is cut.

**Handling**
: Clean hands, an adhesive that suits the backing, and enough length from one batch to keep a wall together.

**Care**
: Dry and gentle. Adhesive on the face marks the weave. Not a washable surface.

[Read the material guide](https://www.mrwallcover.com/materials/grasscloth-and-weaves/) · [Installation service](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/grasscloth-installation/)

## From the first survey to the return visit

The service can cover all five stages, or an agreed installation package. The scope is written for the commission.

### 1. Surveying

We measure on site, or from drawings when that is what the programme needs. Skirting, joinery, doors and windows come out of the area. What is left is the wall that will actually be hung.

Quantities include wastage and pattern repeat. The allowance is typically 15–30%, depending on the pattern repeat and the layout, with staircases at the high end (around 30%) because of the angles. Extra rolls for pattern matching are confirmed at survey. A roll that looks generous on a plan can run short on the last pier.

The survey is also a judgement on the wall. If it is not ready for the material specified, we say so before anyone orders the last drop.

[Read this stage in the service specification](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/#surveying)

### 2. Project management

Every project has a project leader on the ground. That person is the day-to-day contact for the craft, the progress and the expectations that have been set.

On a longer commission, senior oversight quality-controls the work and agrees a forward plan with you, in person, each week. Frequent and plain communication is part of the method, not a courtesy added at the end.

The team you meet on the first day is the core team that finishes. Disruption is kept low: work is contained, floors and joinery are protected, and the place is left tidy every evening. In a home we are visitors. In a hotel we work around the house.

[Read this stage in the service specification](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/#management)

### 3. Supply

On many hotel and flagship jobs the wallcovering is free-issue from the designer, the brand or the client. We manage delivery, check the delivery against the measure, and hang what was specified.

We can also procure. Either way, the order has to cover the repeat and the wastage, or the last wall is the one that waits.

Delivery is timed to the hang. Paper sitting in a corridor for days is a risk to the goods and a nuisance to the building.

[Read this stage in the service specification](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/#supply)

### 4. Installation

This is the craft the practice is built on. Wallcovering installation for hotels, retail flagships and prime residential rooms, including paper on joinery where the drawings show it.

Preparation, where it is in the specification, includes stripping old paper, dustless sanding, and making good cracks and uneven surfaces. Some materials are specified with a lining paper. Some, including certain mural installs, are specified without one. We follow the specification.

Protection of floors, furniture and finished joinery is part of the hang, not an optional extra. Where Mr Wallcover is appointed as main decorating contractor, the package can include fine painting, French polishing and other specialist finishes, as it did at Brown's Hotel, Mayfair.

[Read this stage in the service specification](https://www.mrwallcover.com/services/#installation)

### 5. Aftercare

Aftercare is the fifth part of the service. A return inspection is included once the paper has settled, about four to six weeks after completion. Seams and edges are attended to. There is a priority repair call-out for the workmanship, and a twelve-month guarantee.

### Twelve-month workmanship guarantee

For twelve months from completion, Mr Wallcover guarantees the workmanship of the hang. That covers seams opening, edges lifting, and adhesive failure that comes from the way we hung the material.

It does not cover building movement, damp, leaks, or a substrate that fails. It does not cover damage by later trades, impact, furniture, or cleaning the material cannot take.

Some people call this a warranty. It is the same promise: twelve months on our workmanship, and a clear line where the building takes over from the hang.

Aftercare is one of the five parts of a Mr Wallcover commission. The return visit is included, and the twelve-month workmanship guarantee is set out below.

Return inspection

About four to six weeks after we finish, we come back. By then the heating, the ventilation and the humidity have had a chance to move the paper. That movement is settling. It is the reason for the visit.

We look at seams, edges, corners and any joinery that was covered. Touch-ups that belong to the hang are done on that visit. There is no call-out charge for this inspection.

Seams and edges

A seam that opens, or an edge that lifts, because of the way the material was hung, is our work to put right. We re-adhere, trim and touch in. We do not paint over a problem and hope the light is kind.

Natural seams in grasscloth, silk and hand-painted goods are a property of the material. We will have said so before the hang. Those are not faults. A seam that has let go is different, and we treat it as such.

Priority repair call-out

During the guarantee, a call about our own workmanship comes ahead of new enquiries. The contact page is the quickest way. A photograph helps us see whether we are looking at the hang, a knock, or the building.

If it is the hang, we book the return as a priority. If it is the building, we will say that plainly rather than charge you for a repair that will fail again.

Humidity and settling

Paper and textile move with the moisture in the room. A new hang will ease as it dries, and it may ease again when the heating season starts or when a closed room is opened to damp weather. That is ordinary. It is why the return visit waits four to six weeks, rather than the next morning.

Keep the heat steady and the room ventilated. A cold, damp wall, a leak above a ceiling, or a fan heater pointed at one seam is how edges lift. Do not steam-clean wallcoverings. Do not sit a humidifier against the paper.

If a stain spreads, or a wall feels cold and soft, look for damp before you look for a decorator. We would rather be called early than asked to hang the same wall twice over a wet substrate.

Care and cleaning

This is general guidance for the materials we hang. Where the maker has issued a care note, that note leads. When a mark appears, stop. Household cleaners, steam and magic sponges are how a small mark becomes a large one.

#### Silk

Dust with a clean, dry, soft cloth. No water, no solvent, no steam. A stain needs a conservator, not a detergent. Keep handbags, chair backs and sun off the cloth where you can.

#### Grasscloth and natural weaves

Dry dust only, and lightly. Moisture stains grasscloth and can swell the weave. Seams and variation in colour are part of the material. Do not try to scrub them even.

#### Fabric walling

Vacuum on a low suction with a soft brush head, if the maker allows it. Do not soak the textile. Spot treatment belongs to a specialist, tested somewhere that will not show.

#### Vinyl and contract wallcoverings

Wipe with a barely damp cloth and a drop of mild soap, then dry. No abrasive creams, no neat bleach, no scourers. Contract vinyl will take more than silk. It will not take a careless bucket.

#### Hand-painted papers

No liquids on the surface. Dust dry, and rarely. Direct sun fades a scenic paper unevenly. Marks, flaking and water damage need a paper conservator. We can help you find the right person. We will not experiment on a de Gournay or Fromental-style panel.

#### Murals

Follow the maker's care note for that print or that hand-made piece. Do not use household spray cleaner. If a panel is damaged, photograph it and call us before anyone tries to patch it.

#### Acoustic wallcoverings and panels

Vacuum dust from the face with low suction and a soft head. Do not soak the fabric or the perforations. Water and heavy adhesive at the edge can close the surface the panel was specified to keep open.

For wallcoverings installed by others, send photographs for an assessment of whether a repair is possible. The Mr Wallcover workmanship guarantee applies to its own installation; it does not transfer to an existing hang by another installer.

#### The brief (scope)

To paper the guest rooms and suites: premium finishes, among them Phillip Jeffries' tailored Tweed, that had to sit comfortably beside restored heritage fabric, fine joinery and polished timber, and to read as one surface from the first room to the last.

#### Our approach (approach)

Survey. Every space was surveyed before a single roll was opened, so that substrates, junctions and sight lines were understood rather than discovered.

Preparation and priming. Walls were prepared and primed to suit each material. With textile wallcoverings the substrate is everything; any flaw beneath a tweed or woven surface will eventually show.

Setting out. Drops were planned around windows, doors and joinery so that seams fell where the eye would not look for them, and so that each run began and ended with intent.

Pattern and seam control. Natural textiles vary from roll to roll. Batches, shading and seams were checked continuously so that walls read as one continuous surface.

Coordination. Across the years on site the team worked hand in hand with the electricians, the French polishers and the main contractor, sequencing our work around second-fix electrics and polished joinery so that no trade compromised another. The team's organisation was as much a part of the finish as the craft itself.

#### Outcome (outcome)

Guest room and suite wallpapering from 2016 to 2019 on one of London's most significant hotel conversions, through the opening in 2017 and the rooms that followed. Quiet, tailored walls that let Sir Edwin Cooper's architecture take the applause.

#### At a glance (scope)

- Client: Trematon Castle - Location: Castle of Trematon, above the Lynher and Tamar near Saltash, Cornwall - Years: 2019–2022. Our own site photographs run from 24 April 2019 to 7 January 2022 - Role: Wallcovering installation across the house and estate - Wallcoverings: House of Hackney PLANTASIA, GAIA Prussian Blue, FLORA DELANICA, COSMOS, FLORA FANTASIA, ARTEMIS, HOLLYHOCKS, plus heritage Lincrusta relief - Scope: strip-out and wall preparation, lining, papered ceilings including the atrium ceiling around its oval lantern, large-scale mural setting-out, pattern-matched full-height drops in bedrooms, bathrooms and a powder room, and laser-aligned Lincrusta relief

#### The brief (scope)

To hang House of Hackney wallpapers across the Georgian house. That meant large-scale murals, densely drawn florals and ceiling-to-ceiling "print drenching", in a house where every wall is a little out of true and every ceiling a little out of level.

#### Our approach (approach)

Strip-out and preparation. Our photographs from 2019 and early 2020 show rooms taken back to bare plaster, old finishes scraped off and the atrium stripped and lined. Reinforced lining materials (Wallrock and Anaglypta cartons appear on site in June 2019) were used to give the hand-printed papers a true, stable ground.

Ceilings as a fifth wall. Several rooms were papered overhead. That includes the skylit atrium, where the print had to be cut around a decorative oval lantern and a chandelier rose, and the small box room, papered on all four walls and the ceiling.

Murals and setting-out. PLANTASIA is a panoramic forest landscape that does not forgive an error. Each drop was set out to plan, and photographs from June 2020 show the crew working on ladders across the full height of the room.

Pattern and seam control. Digitally printed papers in deep, saturated colours were batch-checked from the roll labels, and pattern matches were carried across corners, chimney breasts and door heads.

Heritage relief. In November 2021 the team installed Lincrusta, the embossed heritage wallcovering made since 1877. Panels were laid out on the table, then hung to a laser line for perfect vertical alignment.

Living house, long programme. The work ran in phases between 2019 and 2022, around a family home, a working estate and a house that was opening its doors to guests.

#### Outcome (outcome)

A Georgian house within a Norman castle, dressed wall by wall and ceiling by ceiling in the prints of the brand that lives there, across nearly three years of phased work. For a wallcovering contractor there are few sterner tests than hanging a designer's own paper in the designer's own home.

Scope, approach & outcome from the project record

Company-reported project record · [Read the original record](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/old-bailey-hotel/)

#### At a glance (scope)

- Building: 15 Old Bailey, City of London. Grade II listed, completed 1874 - Opened: 2 September 2024 as Hyde London City - Duration: about two years on site (our photographs run from a sample room in November 2022 to final touches in August 2024) - Rooms: 111 guest rooms across seven storeys - Interior design: Studio Moren - Our package: full decoration, specialist wallcoverings and window film - Wallcoverings: Timorous Beasties Tropical Clouded Leopard and Totem Damask (custom-printed vinyl), and House of Hackney LIMERENCE - Window film: Solar Screen

#### The brief (scope)

One team, one standard, from the first sample room to handover: the complete decorating package for 111 guest rooms across seven storeys and the circulation spaces, the specialist wallcoverings that give each room its character, and the window film to the glazing.

#### Our approach (approach)

Sample room first. Our record starts in November 2022 with a finished sample room: Timorous Beasties' Tropical Clouded Leopard set behind the bed as a shaped headboard panel. Agreeing the finish, the setting out and the detailing in one room first meant that the next 110 could be repeated with confidence.

Preparation. A building of this age moves and varies from room to room. New and old surfaces were filled, made good and primed so that the paint and wallcoverings sit on a sound, even base. Our photographs show walls taken back to a smooth finish before a single drop was hung.

Setting out large-scale pattern. Tropical Clouded Leopard is a large-scale, quarter-drop design. Hung as a shaped panel behind each bed, its placement has to be judged room by room so the composition sits centrally and reads as intended. Totem Damask runs differently: a single ten-metre repeat with no horizontal match, so every drop has to be chosen by eye to keep a feature wall balanced.

The suite. In the suite, House of Hackney's LIMERENCE in the Galapagos colourway wraps the room in lush, painterly palms, including the bedroom and the dressing area. It is supplied as four 45 cm panels per roll, so the panels had to be hung in sequence and aligned precisely around joinery and windows.

Window film. Alongside the decoration, we fitted Solar Screen window film to the glazing. See our window film service.

Decoration throughout. The decorating package ran through corridors, stair cores and rooms: deep greens and warm terracottas in the circulation spaces, crisp finishes to the panelling, and careful cutting-in around period mouldings, balustrades and the timber handrail of the main stair.

#### Outcome (outcome)

On 2 September 2024 the first hotel in London to be lit by electricity reopened as a hotel, 150 years after it was built. Guests walk into bedrooms dressed in Timorous Beasties' leopards and kaleidoscopic damasks, a suite wrapped in House of Hackney palms, and corridors and stairs finished to match, all of it delivered by our team.

#### At a glance (scope)

- House: a Grade II-listed Georgian house in North London - Room: a guest bedroom with beams, a built-in writing desk and old, sloping floors - Scheme: House of Hackney's design consultancy - When: April 2019 - Wallpaper: House of Hackney BAMBUSA, Midnight - Our role: wallpaper installation

#### Our work (approach)

Our team hung the BAMBUSA wallpaper. A room like this is unforgiving to paper:

- No straight lines. In an old house, walls lean, floors slope and beams cut across the wall at odd angles. The bamboo stems are strong verticals, so each drop was set out with care to a laser level. The plumb line is the traditional method; the laser gives the same true vertical across the whole room at once, sloping floor and all. That way the forest reads as upright, even though the room around it is not. - Working around period joinery. The paper is cut around the beams, the fireplace, the built-in desk and the shelving, then finished tight to the dark-blue paintwork. - Matching the drama. Midnight is a deep ground. Joins have to be precise and edges clean, because any gap or lifted seam shows against the dark colour.

Our photographs from April 2019 show the room complete: BAMBUSA on every wall, the four-poster dressed, and SERPENTIS overhead.

#### Working airside, in a live terminal (approach)

Airport retail is one of the most tightly controlled places we work. What that means in practice:

- Security first. Airside work means vetted operatives with airside passes. Tools and materials are checked through security and accounted for on site. Deliveries follow the airport's logistics and escort procedures. - Overnight working. In a terminal that never fully closes, the fit-out has to fit around passenger flows. #### Our approach (approach)

- Panel order decides the picture. We checked and sequenced the panels so the horizon runs level across the whole store. - The landscape turns the corner without a break. The mural wraps continuously around internal corners, so the landscape never stops at a wall edge. - Clean lines for the fit-out. The mural meets a lit cove at the top and the joinery at the base. We left it finished around the services, ready for the shop-fitters' fixtures and lighting.

Of these, the overnight working is what our timestamped photographs record for this job. The security and containment points describe what airside work at Heathrow requires in general.
