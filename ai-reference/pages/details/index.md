# Seams, corners, edges and joins, up close

A wallcovering is judged at the seam, in the corner and along the edge. These are our own photographs of those places, taken on finished and in-progress installations, each linked to the project it comes from.

## Seams

Where one drop meets the next. On a tonal or plain ground there is no motif to hide a join, so each seam is butted and rolled until it disappears at normal viewing distance.

![Seam detail in Cadence Oxblood.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/beverly-05-800.webp)

A seam in Cadence Oxblood: a tonal ground, with no motif to hide the join. [Calico Wallpaper × BEVERLY 1975, The Lavery](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/calico-beverly-1975-cadence/)

![Close detail of the entrance-hall wallcovering: soft horizontal bands of charcoal, silver and ochre, laid with no visible seam.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/fs-residences-03-800.webp)

Banded entrance-hall wallcovering in charcoal, silver and ochre, laid with no visible seam. [Four Seasons Private Residences, Ten Trinity Square](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/four-seasons-ten-trinity-square/)

## Internal corners

A corner is where a pattern most often breaks. The paper is carried into the corner and the design is kept continuous across it, so the eye reads one wall, not two.

![The mural wrapped around an internal corner, full height.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/heathrow-05-800.webp)

A Calico Wallpaper landscape mural wrapped round an internal corner, full height. [Calico Wallpaper at Heathrow Terminal 4](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/heathrow-terminal-4-calico/)

![Fromental silk wallcovering in a corner showing seamless pattern match, Four Seasons Hotel London at Ten Trinity Square.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/fs-ig-10-800.webp)

Fromental silk turning into a corner with the pattern matched across it. [Four Seasons Hotel London at Ten Trinity Square](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/four-seasons-ten-trinity-square/)

## Edges

External corners, arches and shadow gaps leave the cut edge on show. The line has to be straight, tight and even along its whole length; where a wall is not true, a bespoke trim makes the edge read as one line.

![A wallcovered arch seen from below, its curved outer edge finished as one crisp, even line, framing a room with a decorative plaster ceiling and a chandelier.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/arch-trim-01-800.webp)

A wallcovered arch, its curved outer edge finished as one crisp, even line. [A prime residence in central London](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/imperfect-arches-external-corner-trim/)

![Close detail of a woven wallcovering on two neighbouring surfaces, each edge cut clean either side of a narrow shadow gap.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/arch-trim-09-800.webp)

Woven wallcovering on two neighbouring surfaces, each edge cut clean either side of a narrow shadow gap. [A prime residence in central London](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/imperfect-arches-external-corner-trim/)

![Close detail of a woven grasscloth wallcovering turning an external corner, the weave running straight and tight to the edge.](https://www.mrwallcover.com/media/img/r/fs-residences-06-800.webp)

Woven grasscloth turning an external corner, the weave straight and tight to the edge. [Four Seasons Private Residences, Ten Trinity Square](https://www.mrwallcover.com/projects/four-seasons-ten-trinity-square/)

## Joins

Where the wallcovering meets something else: a door frame, joinery, a skirting or a switch plate. The cut follows the fitting exactly, with no gap and no overlap.
