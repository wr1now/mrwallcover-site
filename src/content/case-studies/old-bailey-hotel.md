## The building

Old Bailey takes its name from the line of the City's ancient fortified wall, its "bailey", just beyond which the street runs. For centuries the name has been synonymous with justice. The courthouse stood beside Newgate Prison, and by the late nineteenth century, with Newgate dilapidated, both were pulled down to make way for E. W. Mountford's Central Criminal Court, opened by King Edward VII in 1907 and crowned by its gilded Lady of Justice.

Number 15 is older than its famous neighbour. Completed in 1874 by Evans Cronk, the building opened as a hotel, the Spiers & Pond, and is recorded as the first hotel in London to have electric lighting. It later became offices. In 2021 plans were announced to return it to hotel use, and on 2 September 2024 the Grade II-listed building opened as Hyde London City, with 111 bedrooms across seven storeys.

## The brief

To serve as main decorating contractor, taking responsibility not only for installation but for the management and supply of the specialist wallcoverings: a complete chain from specification to the finished wall, inside a listed Victorian building.

## Our approach

**Management.** A single point of responsibility for the wallcovering package: surveying, programming, and coordination with the main contractor and the other trades, so that decoration arrived on site at the right moment, never before the building was ready for it.

**Supply.** Procurement handled from quantities to delivery. As is our practice, quantities are taken from the survey with allowances for pattern repeat and wastage, and deliveries are managed so that batches stay consistent from room to room.

**Preparation and priming.** Walls in a building of this age vary from room to room. Each was prepared and primed to suit the specified material before hanging began.

**Setting out and fit.** Rooms were set out with care around windows, doorcases and the building's period geometry, with seams and pattern placement controlled throughout.
