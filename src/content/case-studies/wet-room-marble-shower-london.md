In March 2019 we prepared the walls and installed a marble-effect Wall&decò WET System in the shower of a private home in London, and worked on other areas of the same bathroom. The home is not named. We photographed every stage, so this page shows what a wet system needs underneath it.

## At a glance

- **Where:** a private residence, London
- **When:** March 2019
- **Scope:** wall preparation and a waterproof wet system in the shower, and other areas of the bathroom
- **Wallcovering:** Wall&decò WET System, marble effect, the generation before AQUABOUT

## Stage 1: new plywood boarding

The shower wall was built new in screwed plywood boards, with a recessed niche box already tanked around its edges and the concealed-cistern frame in place. A wet system shows every flaw under it, which is why we only put one on new plaster, new boarding or new walls, never on existing tiles.

## Stage 2: a reinforced base coat

Plywood is not a surface a wet system goes onto directly. Over the boards went a base coat of a tile-adhesive-type material with a reinforcing mesh bedded into it, much as a microcement bathroom is prepared. That turned the timber into a smooth, solid, mineral wall.

## Stage 3: masked and ready

Every edge, the niche, the flush-plate box and the pipe tails were masked, and so were the joints with the ceiling and the tiled herringbone wall next to the shower. Only then did the system go on.

## Stage 4: the finished shower

The marble-effect drops are butted, with the veining carried across the joints and around the niche. The niche is closed with a black metal trim. Seen from the next room, papered in a large palm-leaf design, the shower reads as one marble room. Every sealant joint, at the tray, the glass screen and the herringbone tiles, is made by a certified sealant specialist. Our May 2021 post ([CPFaYEiFRLw](https://www.instagram.com/p/CPFaYEiFRLw/)) shows the same shower in use two years on.
