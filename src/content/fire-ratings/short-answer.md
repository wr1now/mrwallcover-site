- **A wallcovering's fire rating is a reaction-to-fire class** under BS EN 13501-1, such as B-s1,d0. It describes how the product, tested in a stated build-up, contributes to the early growth of a fire. It is not fire resistance, and it does not give a wall any number of minutes.
- **The class belongs to the tested build-up**: that product, on the substrate and with the adhesive its classification document names, within the variations it permits. A different substrate, adhesive, lining, coating or an old wallcovering underneath can take the installation outside that evidence.
- **In England, Approved Document B Volume 2 sets minimum classes by location** for buildings other than dwellings: D-s3,d2 for small rooms, C-s3,d2 for other rooms and B-s3,d2 for other circulation spaces, where a wallcovering conforming to BS EN 15102 and achieving at least C-s3,d2 is also acceptable if it is bonded to an A2-s3,d2 substrate (note 1 to the table). Dwellings and blocks of flats, including their common areas, are covered by [Volume 1, Table 4.1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa3bb335f6e942efe37f150/AD_B1_2026.pdf): its row for other circulation spaces, including the common areas of flats, is also B-s3,d2 with the same wallcovering note, but its rows for small rooms, garages and circulation spaces within a dwelling differ. It is guidance on meeting the legal requirement, and a project's fire strategy can ask for more.
- **Read the full notation.** "Euroclass B" without s and d values leaves the smoke and droplet results unknown. "Class 0" is an old British national class, with no automatic European equivalent; England stopped using national reaction-to-fire classes in its guidance from 2 March 2025.
- **Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own guidance**, with different tables and, in Wales and Northern Ireland, national classes still printed.
- **Who decides depends on the job.** For building work, the designer, fire engineer or building-control body decides what each wall needs and whether the evidence is enough. For a building in use where the job is not building work, the responsible person under fire-safety law decides, through the fire risk assessment. That law does not apply inside a private home. The manufacturer declares the product's performance. We install the specified product as the maker instructs and record what went on the wall.
