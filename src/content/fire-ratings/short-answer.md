- **A wallcovering's fire rating is a reaction-to-fire class** under BS EN 13501-1, such as B-s1,d0. It describes how the product, tested in a stated build-up, contributes to the early growth of a fire. It is not fire resistance, and it does not give a wall any number of minutes.
- **The class belongs to the tested build-up**: that product, on the substrate and with the adhesive its classification document names, within the variations it permits. A different substrate, adhesive, lining, coating or an old wallcovering underneath can take the installation outside that evidence.
- **In England, Approved Document B Volume 2 sets minimum classes by location**: D-s3,d2 for small rooms, C-s3,d2 for other rooms and B-s3,d2 for other circulation spaces, with a separate route for BS EN 15102 wallcoverings bonded to a class A2-s3,d2 substrate. It is guidance on meeting the legal requirement, and a project's fire strategy can ask for more.
- **Read the full notation.** "Euroclass B" without s and d values leaves the smoke and droplet results unknown. "Class 0" is an old British national class, with no automatic European equivalent; England stopped using national reaction-to-fire classes in its guidance from 2 March 2025.
- **Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own guidance**, with different tables and, in Wales and Northern Ireland, national classes still printed.
- **The designer, fire engineer or building-control body decides** what each wall needs and whether the evidence is enough. The manufacturer declares the product's performance. We install the specified product as the maker instructs and record what went on the wall.
