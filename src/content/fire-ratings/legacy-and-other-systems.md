### Class 0 and Class 1

Before the European classes, British guidance used national classes based on BS 476. **Class 1 to Class 4** were surface spread of flame results under BS 476 Part 7, Class 1 being the best. **Class 0** was not a test result of its own. The guidance defined it: a material of limited combustibility throughout, or a Class 1 material that also met fire propagation limits under BS 476 Part 6 (an index I of no more than 12 and a sub-index i1 of no more than 6). That definition appears in the 2006 edition of Approved Document B and is still printed in the Welsh and Northern Irish guidance.

**There is no automatic conversion** between national classes and European classes. England's guidance said so while it still listed the national classes: products could not assume a European class unless they had been tested for it, and where a smoke or droplet performance was specified, only European classes could be used. Wales and Northern Ireland print the same warning today. A data sheet that says "Class 0" and nothing else is not evidence of any European class.

England removed the national reaction-to-fire classes from its guidance from 2 March 2025, subject to transitional arrangements. BSI shows BS 476 Parts 6 and 7 as withdrawn from 4 March 2025. Neither step, in the documents we read, declares older BS 476 reports invalid; it means the current English guidance is written in European classes only.

### ASTM, IMO and other systems

Some makers sell internationally and print several results side by side. Read each one in its own system:

- **ASTM E84 / UL 723** is a United States tunnel test that reports a flame spread index and a smoke developed index; makers often summarise a result as "Class A". That is not a European class, and US Class A is not class A1 or A2.
- **IMO FTP Code Part 5** is a marine surface-flammability test for ships, with its own criteria. A marine approval is not a building classification.
- **Furniture and curtain tests**, such as BS 5852 for upholstered seating, BS 5867 for curtain fabrics or BS EN 13773 for curtains and drapes, are not evidence for a wallcovering, even when the same textile is used.

For a British project, look for the European classification under BS EN 13501-1 and the build-up it covers. The other results can be useful context; they do not stand in for it.
