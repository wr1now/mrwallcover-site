Building regulations are devolved. The four systems use the same European classes, but their tables, notes and amendment dates differ, and none of them applies in the others. Check the guidance for the place where the building is.

- **Wales** has its own Approved Document B, still based on the 2006 edition with Welsh amendments. Its linings table lists a national class (3, 1 or 0) beside each European class and carries a BS EN 15102 wallcovering note like England's. Wales did not follow England's March 2025 removal of national reaction-to-fire classes; the Welsh Government has said they will move to an appendix at a future revision. Since 1 July 2026 Wales also has dutyholder and competence duties for building work and a procedure for higher-risk buildings ([Building etc. (Amendment) (No. 2) (Wales) Regulations 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2025/1320/contents) and [Building (Higher-Risk Buildings Procedures) (Wales) Regulations 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2025/1321/contents)). These change procedures and duties, not the lining classes. In Wales, a higher-risk building (for design and construction) is at least 18 m high or has at least seven storeys. It must also contain at least one residential unit, a hospital with an overnight bed, a care home or a children's home. A building that is entirely a hotel is excluded.
- **Scotland** uses the building standards technical handbooks, one for domestic and one for non-domestic buildings. The non-domestic handbook's linings table is arranged by building group and by protected or unprotected zone, and uses European classes only. In both handbooks (clause 2.5.1), wall and ceiling surfaces mean the substrate or lining material and exclude decorative wallpaper and paints. Neither handbook defines "decorative wallpaper". Where the work needs a building warrant, agree with the verifier whether a commercial wallcovering, fabric system or panel counts as decoration or as part of the lining. Work that needs no warrant must still meet the building regulations: no verifier checks it, and the owner or client has the duty to comply. In a hotel, office or other building covered by fire-safety law, the fire risk assessment should also take it into account. The main fire-safety duties exclude homes and the shared stairs and passages of flats, although a narrow duty to maintain facilities for firefighters still applies to the shared parts. The same clause says multiple layers can increase flame spread and are not recommended when redecorating.
- **Northern Ireland** uses Technical Booklet E. Its table gives both national and European classes by purpose group, room size and circulation space, and it has no separate wallcovering note.

In Wales and Northern Ireland, where national classes are still printed, the guidance itself says national and European classes do not automatically equate. Wales applies the Building Regulations 2010 as amended for Wales, with its own approved documents; Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own building regulations.
