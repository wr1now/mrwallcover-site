A wallcovering's European class comes from a small family of standards. BS EN 13501-1 is the classification standard: it turns test results into a class. The tests themselves are the single burning item test (BS EN 13823) and a small-flame ignitability test (BS EN ISO 11925-2). The product is mounted for testing on a standard substrate chosen under BS EN 13238, and decorative wallcoverings declare their performance under the product standard BS EN 15102.

Two cautions when reading a document:

- **The latest edition is not always the edition that applies.** A report or declaration cites the edition used when it was written, and the edition cited for UKCA and CE marking can lag behind BSI's latest. BS EN 15102 is the clearest case: BSI lists a 2019 edition, while the designated-standards lists and England's guidance cite 2007+A1:2011. Record what the document cites; do not silently substitute a newer edition.
- **A catalogue record is not the standard.** We have checked titles, editions and status in BSI's catalogue. We have not reproduced the paid texts, and nothing on this page should be read as a summary of their clauses.

Under the construction products rules, a declaration of performance states the class the manufacturer declares for the product. Where no value is declared for a characteristic, the declaration shows "NPD": the regulation spells it out as no performance determined, and some declarations print it as no performance declared. Either way it is not a class and not a pass.
