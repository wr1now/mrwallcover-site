We read the fire documents that Muraspec, Tektura, Vescom, Arte International and Newmor publish for a selection of their commercial wallcoverings. The table shows one row per product or product family, the class exactly as printed, the kind of document it comes from, the build-up behind it and the gaps we found. It is not a list of approved products, and a row says nothing about a maker's other products.

What the documents showed, across all five makers:

- **The class is tied to a test board.** Most declarations and reports name fibre-cement or calcium silicate board and a particular adhesive. Several limit the result to dense mineral boards, and some expressly exclude paper-faced plasterboard. Check the substrate line before assuming a class applies to your wall.
- **One maker, several classes.** Every maker we looked at publishes different classes for different products, including some at class C or D. A collection name or brand is not a classification.
- **Sheets and declarations do not always agree.** We found technical sheets that give a class without subclasses, product pages that give a different class from the sheet, declarations that cite a report with a different number or class, and older and newer declarations that give different smoke subclasses for the same product type. Where two documents disagree, the row shows both.
- **The type of document matters.** A declaration of performance or a classification report from a named laboratory carries more weight than a technical sheet or a web page, which usually gives no test details at all. For some products only a sheet was published.
- **Dates are printed in different ways.** Some certificates and reports state a validity date; a few of those dates have passed. Others state no period. We record what each document says and flag a printed date that has passed; we do not invent one where none is given.

Before ordering, ask the maker for the current declaration of performance and the classification report for the exact product and backing, and check that the substrate and adhesive in that report match the wall you have.
