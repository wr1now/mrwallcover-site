Several parties are involved, and none of them can stand in for another.

- **The test laboratory and classification body.** A laboratory tests the product in a stated build-up to the European test methods. A classification report then gives the class and records the build-up and the field of application the result covers. Depending on the product and its system of assessment, a notified or approved body may also be involved.
- **The manufacturer.** It draws up the declaration of performance for the product, takes responsibility for that declaration, and publishes the installation instructions. Its technical sheet and website summarise the position; when they disagree with the declaration or the classification report, the formal document is the one to check and the disagreement is a question for the maker.
- **The designer, fire engineer and building-control body.** For building work, they decide what each wall and ceiling has to achieve and accept or reject the evidence for the product proposed. For a variation from the documented build-up, England's Approved Document B expects test evidence or an assessment by an organisation with the necessary expertise, not just a letter. On a project with a fire strategy, that strategy sets the requirement.
- **The responsible person, for a building in use.** In England and Wales this is the "responsible person" under the Fire Safety Order (article 3): the employer, for a workplace under its control; otherwise whoever controls the premises for a business or other undertaking; or else the owner. Anyone else with some control shares the duties for what they control, including anyone whose contract or tenancy makes them responsible for maintenance, repair or safety, such as a landlord or managing agent (article 5). Scotland and Northern Ireland do not use the term. There the duties fall on employers, on anyone with control of the premises, in some cases on the owner, and on anyone with such a contract or tenancy. See the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005, sections 53 and 54, and the Fire and Rescue Services (Northern Ireland) Order 2006, articles 25 and 26. This page says "responsible person" for all of them. Where redecorating is not building work, no building-control body approves it: the responsible person decides, through the fire risk assessment, often with a fire risk assessor. In England and Wales, if building work makes a significant change to a building in use, they must also review that assessment (article 9(3)).
- **The installer.** We install the specified product with the specified adhesive and preparation, as the maker's instructions set out, and keep a record of what was installed. We do not test, classify or certify a product's reaction to fire. If the wall, the adhesive or the product on site differs from the specification, we stop and raise it before hanging.

An adhesive sold as containing a fire retardant, or a lining paper with its own class, does not classify the finished wall. Neither does a "contract" or "commercial" label on a sample book.
