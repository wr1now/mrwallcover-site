A classification describes the build-up that was tested and the variations its classification document expressly allows. Before ordering, check that what will actually go on the wall matches it on each of these points:

- **The exact product.** Maker, collection, product reference and, where the document lists them, the colourways or designs it covers. A collection name alone is not enough when one collection mixes constructions.
- **Construction and backing.** Vinyl on non-woven, vinyl on paper or woven backing, textile, or a natural material. A change of backing is a different product for fire purposes.
- **Weight and finish.** Documents often state a weight or weight range. Embossing, metallic or flock finishes and protective top coats can change the result.
- **Printed and bespoke versions.** A digitally printed design is a print on a named base material with named inks. Its evidence is the evidence for that base and that process, not the evidence for a different stock product in the same range.
- **Adhesive and primer.** The adhesive is part of the tested build-up. Use the adhesive the document names. Treat a different adhesive, even a good one, as a change to the tested build-up: unless the classification document already covers it, it needs the maker's written position and test or assessment evidence that the project professional accepts.
- **Substrate.** Classification reports name the board the product was tested on, often fibre-cement or calcium silicate board, and say which end-use substrates the result also covers. Several reports we read limit that to dense mineral boards of a stated density and class, and some expressly exclude paper-faced plasterboard. A plasterboard, plaster or painted wall is covered only as far as the classification document says.
- **Lining paper.** Hanging over lining paper adds a layer to the build-up. A lining paper's own classification does not classify the wallcovering hung over it.
- **Fixing method and any air gap.** Panels, stretched systems or wallcoverings over battens or a cavity are a different build-up from a wallcovering bonded to a solid wall.

### Common questions on site

**Can it go over existing wallpaper?** Assume not. Hanging over an old wallcovering puts an untested layer in the build-up. [Muraspec's](https://www.muraspec.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Muraspec-UK-Hanging-Instructions-May-26.pdf) and [Vescom's](https://documents.vescom-cdn.com/f1873/EN%20-%20Algemeen%20verwerkingsvoorschrift%20-%20Vescom%20vinyl%2C%20olefin%20en%20textiel%20wandbekleding%202025.pdf) hanging instructions ask for existing wallcoverings and backing papers to be removed, and [Dreamscape](https://dreamscapewalls.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/12000015609-can-i-install-wallcovering-over-existing-wallcovering-pvc-on-pvc), a vinyl wallcovering maker, says that installing vinyl over an existing wallcovering voids its product's fire rating. Check the wallpaper maker's guide.

**Over lining paper?** Only where the maker's instructions and its classification evidence cover it, or a test or assessment report covers that build-up, and the project professional accepts it.

**Over timber, MDF or plywood?** A combustible board is a different substrate from plasterboard or masonry. Ask the maker whether its classification covers it, and ask the project's fire specialist what the lining as a whole needs to achieve.

**Over plasterboard, plaster or paint?** These are the walls most projects have, and they are not always the substrate the product was tested on. Check what the classification document says about end-use substrates before ordering. For preparation, follow the maker's instruction; on gloss or other sealed surfaces a vinyl-over-vinyl adhesive is a different adhesive from the one in most tests.

**A different paste, or a seam and corner adhesive?** Ask the maker first. A small amount of overlap or border adhesive is still a change to the specified system.

**A protective coating, varnish or anti-graffiti film added later?** That adds a layer. It needs the maker's agreement and, where the area carries a classification requirement, its own evidence.

We do not offer improvised fixes for fire performance. Where the proposed build-up is outside what the documents cover, ask the manufacturer for its written position. England's guidance expects a variation to be covered by a test, or by an assessment from an organisation with the necessary expertise, and the responsible project professional decides whether the evidence is enough before installation.
