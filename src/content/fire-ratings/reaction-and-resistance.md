**Reaction to fire** is how much a product contributes, by its own decomposition, to a fire under specified test conditions. It is what a wallcovering's class describes: how readily it ignites, how fast it releases heat and spreads flame in the early stages of a fire, how much smoke it gives off in the test and whether it drops flaming particles. England's Approved Document B uses that definition in Appendix B.

**Fire resistance** is a different property, measured for building elements: how long a wall, floor, door or ceiling continues to do its job in a fire, for example keeping fire and heat on one side. It is expressed in minutes under a separate classification standard, BS EN 13501-2, with notations such as EI 30 or EI 60. A wallcovering has no fire-resistance period of its own, and hanging one does not give a wall 30, 60 or 120 minutes. If a wall needs fire resistance, that comes from the wall's construction and its own evidence.

Three more limits are worth stating plainly:

- **The smoke subclass is not a toxicity rating.** s1, s2 and s3 describe how much smoke the product produced in the single burning item test, judged by how it obscured light. They say nothing about how toxic the smoke is. England's guidance for linings does not deal with smoke and fumes at all, so a smoke requirement, if there is one, comes from the project.
- **No class means "safe anywhere".** A classification is a test result for a defined build-up. Whether it is enough depends on where the product goes and what the project requires. Words such as "fireproof", "non-flammable" or "zero smoke" on a wallcovering should send you looking for the classification document.
- **Class F, "no performance determined" and "unclassified" are three different things.** Class F is a class: it is given when a product was tested and did not achieve class E. "No performance determined" (NPD) on a declaration of performance means the manufacturer has not declared a value for that characteristic. "Unclassified" or "untested" means there is no classification at all: under Approved Document B, an untested product cannot be classified to BS EN 13501-1. None of the three should be read as a pass, and none should be read as a known failure beyond what the document says.
