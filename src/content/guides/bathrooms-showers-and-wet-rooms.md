Can a bathroom have wallpaper, even inside the shower? Yes, but not the same wallpaper everywhere. Inside the shower you need a purpose-made **waterproof wet system**. We have installed Wall&decò's WET System in many bathrooms, mainly in central London, with supply through West One Bathrooms, and Glamora's waterproof system too. Outside the water, decorative papers can work, within each maker's own limits. This guide explains which goes where, which walls a wet system needs, and how we detail and hand over a wet-system shower.

## Which wallcovering can go where in a bathroom?

Think of the room as three areas. This is our own way of explaining it, not the electrical zoning used for lights and sockets.

- **Inside the shower, or wherever water hits the wall:** a wet system only. Ordinary wallpaper is not waterproof.
- **Beside basins and baths, and outside a shower screen:** a product its maker rates for damp areas, in a room with good ventilation. [Cole & Son](https://cole-and-son.com/en-us/pages/frequently-asked-questions) allows its non-woven papers in a well-ventilated bathroom, as long as they are not in direct contact with water. [Fromental](https://fromental.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions) allows its papers where there is good ventilation and little direct water, and warns that silk may not suit a steamy bathroom. A factory glaze helps, but [de Gournay](https://degournay.com/uploads/technical/document/Protective_Finish_Options_-_Wallpaper_-_English2.pdf) says its standard glaze stops well short of waterproof.
- **The rest of the room, cloakrooms and powder rooms:** decorative papers chosen with the maker's limits in mind. Makers disagree here: some allow their papers in ventilated bathrooms and some do not, so the sheet for the exact product decides.

The [choosing guide](/advice/choosing-wallcoverings/) covers the same idea for the whole house.

## What is a wet system?

A wet system is not a wallpaper with a coating. Wall&decò's WET System is a sealed build-up made on the wall:
1. a water-tanking primer;
2. a dedicated adhesive;
3. a printed fibreglass fabric that carries the design;
4. a clear protective finish over the top.

The design is printed to the size of the wall, so a mural can run straight into the shower. Wall&decò's [installation guidelines](https://www.wallanddeco.com/en-us/commons/product-installation) ask for an installer who specialises in wallpaper and who follows the manual's sequence, quantities and drying times exactly. [West One Bathrooms](https://westonebathrooms.com/inspiration/all/waterproof-wallpaper/) shows the system in its London showrooms.

Two things we check on every job:
- **Which generation is supplied.** Wall&decò's current generation, AQUABOUT, uses a different finishing sequence from the earlier WET System that we have installed. The two procedures are never mixed.
- **Which maker it is.** Glamora's waterproof system is a thin, pre-finished sheet, not a site-built layer. Inside a shower cubicle, Glamora asks for a waterproofing coat on the wall first.

## Which walls will we put a wet system on?

**New plaster, new boarding or new walls. Never existing tiles.** Wall&decò itself allows tiles once they are skimmed or overboarded, but a wet system shows every flaw underneath it, so we start from a new, sound, smooth, dry wall.

Where a shower wall is built new in **plywood**, we first apply a base coat of a tile-adhesive-type material with a reinforcing mesh bedded into it, much as a microcement bathroom is prepared. That turns timber boarding into a solid, smooth, mineral surface. Only then does the wet system go on. Our [marble-effect shower case study](/projects/wet-room-marble-shower-london/) shows each stage in photographs: bare plywood, the prepared wall, masking, hanging, and the shower in use.

The general rules for any wall are in the [wall preparation guide](/advice/wall-preparation/).

## How do we detail the tray, niches, screens and windows?

- **Joints between drops:** butted, with the design running continuously across them.
- **Niches:** we line them, and every niche is closed with a metal trim.
- **Window reveals:** common in bathrooms, and we always wrap them, as in our [Belgravia bathroom](/projects/wet-room-stone-effect-bathroom-belgravia/).
- **Shower tray:** Wall&decò stops the fabric a few millimetres short of the tray, and the gap is then sealed.
- **Tray, glass screens and tile edges:** every sealant joint is made by a **certified sealant specialist**, never by the paperhanger. We have one in our team who does nothing else.

A fault in a wet system is very hard to put right, so we plan every step two or three times before we start.

## When can the shower be used?

After about **two days** on our jobs. The system keeps curing for **seven to fourteen days**, and the shower can be used during that time. Wall&decò's own instructions put full drying at around 48 hours in the right conditions.

## Who measures, and how is it supplied?

Usually West One Bathrooms or another specialist measures the walls. On some jobs we measure and supply our own survey. Wall&decò prints each design to the measured wall, on rolls 94 cm wide made to the wall's height, and the order includes the system's own primer, adhesive and finishes.

## When does this guide stop being reliable?

It explains how we choose and install wallcoverings in bathrooms. The hanging instructions for the exact product supplied always decide the method. Waterproofing behind tiles, trays and screens, and the room's ventilation, belong to the bathroom design and the builder. For a bathroom, send photographs of the room, its size, the product or design you have in mind, and where the shower, bath and basin sit.
