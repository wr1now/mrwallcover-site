Dividing wall area by the area of a roll can give a misleading answer. Wallpaper is usually installed in full drops, and the pattern may determine where each drop begins.

## Width decides how many drops you need

For a simple rectangular wall, usable material width helps determine the number of drops. Height, trimming allowance and pattern matching then influence how much length each drop uses. Roll length determines how many usable drops can be cut from one roll.

Consider a deliberately simplified example: a wall 3 metres wide and 2.4 metres high, with plain wallpaper 0.5 metres wide and a 10-metre roll. Six full-width drops cover the wall. Allowing 0.1 metres in total for trimming gives a 2.5-metre cut length, so four drops fit in a roll and two rolls are needed. This assumes exactly usable dimensions, no defects, no additional pattern allowance and a straightforward layout. It is an illustration, not an ordering instruction.

## Pattern changes the calculation

A repeat is not a universal waste percentage. Straight matches, offset matches and large patterns can use material differently. The start position may also matter if the design needs to sit at a particular height.

Murals and panoramic papers need their own panel plan. They should not be treated as ordinary rolls with a percentage added.

## Openings do not always save a full drop

A window removes wall area, but that does not mean its entire area can be deducted from the order. You may still need lengths above and below it, with the correct pattern relationship. Offcuts are only useful if their size and pattern position suit the remaining work.

Before ordering, check usable width, length, repeat, match, batch and all wall dimensions. Difficult geometry, stairwells and bespoke designs deserve professional review. Keep purchasing quantity separate from any labour measurement used in a quotation.
