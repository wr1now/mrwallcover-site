A wallcovering package becomes difficult when decisions, materials and site readiness arrive in the wrong order. Planning should connect all three before installation is booked across multiple rooms or floors.

## Build a room-by-room schedule

List the areas, material references, drawing revisions, quantities and required dates. Include access restrictions and whether the building remains occupied. A corridor with continuous guest traffic needs a different working arrangement from an empty apartment.

## Define the handover conditions

Agree what “ready for wallcovering” means for the specified product and substrate. Identify who checks the walls, how issues are recorded and how unresolved preparation affects the programme. Sequence the work with wet trades, joinery and other activities that could damage the finish.

## Confirm the documents

Where the project sets fire, environmental or other performance requirements, obtain current evidence for the exact proposed product and relevant installation conditions. Ask the responsible project professional to confirm acceptance. A general label such as “contract wallcovering” is not evidence that every requirement has been met.

## Manage delivery and changes

Decide who receives, checks and stores the material. Record batches, room allocation and any panel sequence. If a substitute is proposed, review appearance, installation implications and required documents before approving it.

Use an agreed sample area or mock-up where the scope calls for one. Record acceptance before repeating the work across the building.

## Plan protection and handover

Finished walls need protection from subsequent trades. Agree responsibility, snagging arrangements and the handover information, including care instructions and any retained material for future repairs.

For tender or procurement, provide scope, elevations, material schedules, programme, access rules and known preparation conditions. A properly defined package is easier to compare and easier to deliver.
