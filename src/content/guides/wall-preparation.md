Wallpaper does not repair the wall underneath it. An expensive finish can make an uneven surface more noticeable, particularly under side lighting or with a smooth, reflective material.

## What needs checking

The surface needs to be suitable for the chosen system. That means looking at stability, dryness, smoothness and the behaviour of any existing coating. A wall can look sound while an old layer of paint or filler remains poorly bonded underneath.

Photographs are useful for planning, but they do not answer every question. Where the condition is uncertain, an on-site inspection or appropriate test area may be needed before confirming the scope.

## Preparation is a sequence

Stripping, repairs, sanding, lining and priming may all form part of the work, but not every wall needs the same treatment. The correct sequence depends on the substrate and the wallcovering manufacturer's instructions. There is no single sealer or adhesive that solves every situation.

Fresh repairs and plaster need suitable conditions and time before they are covered. A calendar date by itself is not proof that the surface is ready. Moisture concerns should be investigated, rather than hidden behind a new finish.

## Plan it into the programme

If preparation is discovered late, it can change the installation date. That becomes more difficult when deliveries, decorators, joinery and occupancy have already been arranged.

Ask for preparation to be described separately in the quotation. Where something cannot be confirmed until stripping or inspection, record that uncertainty and agree how additional work will be authorised.

The best preparation is rarely the most visible part of a finished room. It is still one of the main reasons the finish has a good chance of performing as intended.
