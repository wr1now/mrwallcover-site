A wallcovering package becomes difficult when decisions, materials and site readiness arrive in the wrong order. For tender or procurement, provide scope, elevations, material schedules, programme, access rules and known preparation conditions. A properly defined package is easier to compare and easier to deliver.

## Scope

List the areas, material references, drawing revisions, quantities and required dates. Include access restrictions and whether the building remains occupied.

## Measurement basis

State whether quantities are measured net of openings, and who confirms the purchase quantity.

## Substrate handover

Agree what “ready for wallcovering” means for the specified product and substrate. Identify who checks the walls, how issues are recorded and how unresolved preparation affects the programme.

## Programme and changes

Sequence the work with wet trades, joinery and other activities that could damage the finish. If a substitute is proposed, review appearance, installation implications and required documents before approving it.

## Snagging and spares

Finished walls need protection from subsequent trades. Agree responsibility, snagging arrangements and the handover information, including care instructions and any retained material for future repairs.
