Whatever the product's own instruction names, and the instructions differ widely. de Gournay asks for every wall to be lined; Farrow & Ball and the Sanderson Design Group brands recommend cross-lining; Phillip Jeffries says lining is not a necessity with most of its collections; Vescom's specification has no lining step at all. This guide goes deeper than [Is the wall ready for wallpaper?](/advice/wall-preparation/), which explains what lining, sizing and paste are for. The instruction for the product on your wall always comes first.

## What do lining grades and weights mean?

A grade is a lining paper's thickness class: the higher the number, the more it hides. Erfurt MAV describes each grade of its [Professional range](https://www.erfurtmav.com/erfurt-mav-wallpaper-products/accessories/product/499-professional-lining-paper-1400-grade) by the wall it suits: 800 for new plaster, 1000 for older plaster in good condition, 1200 and 1400 for minor and larger cracks, 1700 up to Supercover 2500 for damaged plaster. Non-woven and reinforced liners are specified by weight in g/m² instead: Erfurt's flat Variovlies grades from about 120 to 200, its R 300 PRO renovation liner about 280, Wallrock Fibreliner 150 or 210. The maker of the finish names which, as the table shows.

## Paper or non-woven liner: what is the difference?

A paper liner is pasted, folded and soaked, and it grows as it does: by "as much as 10mm", Anaglypta says. A non-woven liner goes up dry, into paste on the wall. Erfurt's [Variovlies guide](https://www.erfurt.com/fileadmin/download/service/Folder/Variovlies_Handbuch_Profi_GB.pdf) says that is possible "due to their dimensional stability", and its data sheets list "no soaking", "covers cracks" and "easy to remove" among the benefits; its R 300 PRO sheet adds that dry-strippable wallcoverings come off it.

Neither suits everything. de Gournay warns that the liner must be water-absorbent, and "many problem solving liner papers are not". Erfurt rates its own non-wovens separately for being papered over: V 130, V 150 and V 180 "recommended", the M grades and V 200 S "limited suitability". The finish's instruction chooses the liner.

## What is cross-lining, and when is it left out?

Cross-lining is hanging the lining horizontally, so its joints cross the joints of the finish and never sit beneath them. Farrow & Ball, Cole & Son, Little Greene and the Sanderson Design Group brands recommend it. de Gournay says it "is not necessary" so long as lining and panel joints do not meet, and Fromental allows either direction. Under double-cut papers makers disagree: Timorous Beasties advises against cross-lining "due to the risk of slicing the lining paper, which can cause seams to split", while Lewis & Wood asks for cross-lining under its double-cut wide-width paper.

Either way, lining is butt-jointed, never overlapped, and dry before the finish goes on: at least 12 hours for Farrow & Ball, overnight for Phillip Jeffries' [Aviator](https://api.phillipjeffries.com/files/instructions/AV.pdf), normally 24 hours for Wallrock Fibreliner. de Gournay also asks for it to be hung with the same adhesive as its panels.

## What do we line Fromental's non-woven-backed silks with?

For Fromental's non-woven-backed silks, we line the wall with Erfurt's 100 cm wide non-woven lining. That is our own practice and standard for those silks, not a Fromental rule: Fromental's guide names neither Erfurt nor a non-woven liner.

Fromental's [installation guide for non-woven backed silk](https://web.archive.org/web/20231130182143/https://fromental.co.uk/cdn/shop/files/Nonwoven_paper_backed_silk_Installation_Guide.pdf?v=17284818687869208217) (2022, now an archived copy) says the walls "need to be lined with thousand grade lining paper" (blank stock in the USA), sized first with a PVA-based size, and clean and white because "the silk wallcoverings are slightly translucent". It asks for a thick ready-mixed starch tub paste, undiluted (Murabond 'black top' or Albany Yellow top in the UK), on the wall; the back of each panel is sponged and left 1–2 minutes. Erfurt's own sheets say what its non-woven does: it goes up on a pasted wall without soaking, and covers cracks. Fromental's [current website](https://fromental.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions) says installation details come from its sales representative; on a live order, that instruction comes first.

## When is a size enough, and when does the wall need a primer?

We prime the walls with a good primer before hanging, where the product's instruction allows it; the maker's instruction comes first. A size is thinned paste that evens out suction (Lincrusta: "diluted regular wallpaper adhesive"); a wallpaper primer also strengthens or seals the surface. Zinsser says its Gardz protects plasterboard when the covering is stripped, and Vescom that its primer, used neat, makes later removal easier. Henkel, maker of Metylan, put it plainly in 2013: paste where only absorbency needs regulating, a strengthening wallpaper primer where more is needed, and no [primers from paint systems](https://www.henkel.com/resource/blob/191636/7fa7229ba4f48e6690996c09420a8984/data/2013-10-07-presseinformation-konzentration-auf-das-wesentliche-pdf.pdf).

The dilution is the paste maker's: Little Greene says to add 10–15% water, the Albany Super Smooth label an equal quantity, and Henkel gives 1:20 for Metylan NP as a size. New plaster is "extremely porous but also extremely alkaline", in Zinsser's words. Cole & Son asks for two or three weeks' drying or a size, and Zinsser UK says [Gardz](https://www.zinsseruk.com/products/gardz), rated alkali-resistant to pH 12.5, goes on plaster cured at least 24 hours. Where PVA appears in a system it is diluted: Fromental's size is "PVA based", Erfurt MAV dilutes its Wallrock PVA sealer one part to three of water, and Bartoline's own data sheet thins its PVA 1:4 and does not list wallpaper among its uses.

## Which primers do makers ask for, and which do they rule out?

Pigmented primers are asked for mostly under naturals, wood veneers and vinyls. Phillip Jeffries' sheets ask for "a hard, acrylic, pigmented, wall-protecting primer" made for wallcovering, and its own guide adds "never a paint primer"; Thibaut wants a universal pre-wallcovering primer, Calico a wallpaper-specific one, Omexco an acrylic basecoat on boards. Colour matters too. Timorous Beasties: "Avoid white lining paper for dark wallpapers. Paint wall with tinted primer that coordinates with wallpaper." Maya Romanoff's primer is tinted close to the product and Dedar recommends a surface painted the colour of the wallcovering; under silk, Fromental wants it "clean and white". A clear sealer will not give that: Rust-Oleum's [Gardz data sheet](https://www.rustoleum.com/-/media/20879290034B43B58B6563DE2E611CC6.ashx) says "translucent wallcoverings may require an additional coat of an opaque primer".

Some instructions rule primer out. de Gournay says "Do not use a primer on top of the lining paper" and bars oil-based primers that stay flexible. Maya Romanoff's Precious Metals take no sealing primer, and Calico rules out Roman R-35 Pro-935, which [Roman describes](https://romandecoratingproducts.com/collections/wallpaper-primers) as a heavy-tack primer for tile and glass. de Gournay also warns that lime plaster, concrete and mould-inhibiting coatings tarnish metal leaf and speed fading on silk grounds; Cole & Son asks for an alkali-resistant primer under foils; British Gypsum says vinyl and other low-permeability wallcoverings should not go straight onto board treated with its [Drywall Sealer](https://web.archive.org/web/20240617105417/https://www.british-gypsum.com/technical-support/self-help-tools/faqs/how-do-you-apply-wallpaper-plasterboard), because they restrict the drying of water-based adhesives.

## Which paste suits which wallcovering?

The one the maker names: pastes differ in base, strength, water content and pH.

- **Powder and flake pastes**, mixed with water, such as Henkel's methylcellulose Metylan Vlies for non-wovens. The ratio follows the wallcovering; de Gournay and Fromental steer away from self-mix pastes, Cole & Son from "packet mixes".
- **Ready-mixed tub pastes**, starch-based and often blended with PVA, such as [Albany Super Smooth](https://www.brewers.co.uk/uploads/datasheets/products/202334.pdf?v=1665669000) and [Bartoline Extra Strong Professional](https://bartoline.co.uk/product_items/ready-mixed-adhesive-professional/), which de Gournay names as UK alternatives to its own.
- **Heavy-duty and speciality pastes** for textiles and heavier backings: Albany's red-lid Speciality lists silks, grasscloths and linens; [Murabond](https://www.muraspec.com/product/heavy10kg/) comes Heavy for fabric-backed and Light for paper-backed vinyl; Vescom specifies its own by type and weight.
- **Clear adhesives** for naturals and silks: Phillip Jeffries, Thibaut and Omexco ask for clear pre-mixed ones.
- **Clay adhesives**: Phillip Jeffries specifies one for its [wood veneers](https://api.phillipjeffries.com/files/instructions/AGAINST%20THE%20GRAIN%204.3.26.pdf), and Fromental prefers one under linen "if available".
- **System adhesives** for one product: Erfurt's SR 2 PRO or SR 4 PRO under its R 300 PRO liner, with the finish then hung on its own paste, "at the very least special adhesive at a ratio of 1:20"; Lincrusta recommends no adhesive but its own.

Read the paste's own data sheet against the instruction. Farrow & Ball asks for pH 7 to 12 because "acidic pastes can damage the wallpaper"; Little Greene's own paste, made for its papers, is pH 6.0. Timorous Beasties asks for a paste containing a fungicide, while Sanderson says its pastes have none added, only in-container preservatives. And tub pastes for fine papers are not thinned: Fromental says "do not dilute the paste!", and Phillip Jeffries warns that water added under silk can pull the backing from the face.

## Paste the wall or the paper, and how long does it soak?

The product's label decides; the backing alone does not. Most non-wovens go on a pasted wall: Cole & Son says most of its papers are "non-woven paste the wall products". Most paper-backed papers, and Phillip Jeffries' naturals, are pasted on the back. But House of Hackney's traditional non-woven is pasted on the back and its woven-backed digital prints on the wall, and Fromental's silk goes onto a pasted wall after its back is sponged. Pierre Frey's guides have paste rolled about 10 cm wider than the drop, with less at the seam, because "excess paste can spill out on the front".

Soaking is the maker's figure too. Erfurt MAV gives its lining papers 6–8 minutes at 800 grade, rising to 10–12 at the heavy grades; non-woven liners and Wallrock Fibreliner need none. For finishes:

## Why is the adhesive part of the fire-tested build-up?

Because a fire classification describes a wallcovering on a stated substrate with a stated adhesive, not the roll alone. Arte's [classification report](https://edge.arte-international.com/media/2ee4d956-0c3e-4816-8cd9-d725b0c99db0/2.-Wallcovering-with-natural-materials.pdf) for its natural wallcoverings, issued by Centexbel in September 2026, gives B-s1,d0 for a field of application of "starch glue applied at the rate of 200 gr/m²" on substrates of at least 1350 kg/m³ classified A2-s1,d0 or A1. Outside that, the report does not cover the wall. Liners carry classes too: Erfurt's sheets give its Makulatur C-s1,d0 and R 300 PRO D-s1,d0, and Erfurt MAV says its class E [Thermal Liner](https://www.erfurtmav.com/erfurt-mav-wallpaper-products/wallrock/wallrock-thermal-liner) is not for projects "where Building regulations are applicable". So a substitute paste, primer or liner needs its own evidence; the [hotel fire safety guide](/advice/hotel-fire-safety-and-compliance/) explains who certifies what.

## When does this advice stop being reliable?

This is a reading of makers' published documents, checked on 11 October 2026, and they change: Fromental's silk guides have left its website, and de Gournay keeps separate instructions for papers made before 2026. The instruction in the roll or with the order takes precedence. Makers limit claims once hanging starts (Phillip Jeffries and Omexco after three strips, Pierre Frey after three drops), so the first drops are checked before the rest. Lime plaster, historic fabric, damp and walls with a fire or acoustic requirement need the product's instruction and, often, the specifier's confirmation.
