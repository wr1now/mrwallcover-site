You do not need a complete specification to start a conversation. Send what you know about the rooms and the material, and mark anything that still needs a decision. These notes help us prepare for the survey; they are not a substitute for seeing the walls.

## The rooms and the location

- The project location and which rooms or areas are included.
- Approximate widths and heights, or current drawings if you have them. Say what has been measured and what is approximate.
- Whether the room is a home, a hotel or another working building, and whether it will be occupied during the work.

## The chosen wallcovering

- The maker, collection, product code and colourway, with the hanging instructions where available.
- Whether the paper is already ordered, whether it is from one batch and who supplies it.
- What is still undecided. If you are choosing, the [materials library](/materials/) explains the families and what they ask of the room.

The product reference tells us more than a description: usable width, pattern repeat, trimmed or untrimmed edges, backing and any panel sequence all affect the work. A scenic panel needs its own set-out; it cannot be treated as an interchangeable roll.

## Photographs and access

- Photograph each wall square-on, including corners, doors, windows and any joinery the covering will meet.
- For a staircase, show it from below and from the landing, with the ceiling height if known.
- Include access constraints: lifts, common parts, parking, high walls or a stair void. Say what furniture and finished surfaces need protection.

## Preparation and timing

- Describe the existing paper or paint and any known damage, damp history or recent plastering. The [wall preparation guide](/advice/wall-preparation/) explains what needs inspection.
- Give your preferred timing and any work that must finish first. Include permitted working hours or hotel room-release restrictions where relevant.

Preparation may include stripping old paper, filling, sanding, priming or lining where the wall and the specification require it. What can only be confirmed after stripping or inspection should be recorded as uncertain, with a way to agree any extra work.

## What the survey settles

The survey confirms the wall measurements, wall readiness, access and the material's set-out needs. Openings are taken out of the labour measure; the purchase quantity separately allows for full drops, repeat and wastage. A large repeat, a drop match or a room broken up by openings can change both the material order and the time on site.

After the survey we write a scope of works by room. The quotation says what has been allowed for, including access, protection and making good. Every job is quoted after survey; Mr Wallcover publishes no price list or flat rate per roll or square metre.

Aftercare is included: a return visit about four to six weeks after completion and twelve months' cover on the workmanship of the hang. The [aftercare page](/aftercare/) explains the care advice and guarantee limits.
