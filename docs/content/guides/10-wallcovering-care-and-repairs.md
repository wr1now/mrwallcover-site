# Caring for wallpaper, and what to do when something goes wrong
The right care starts with knowing which wallcovering was installed. Keep the product reference and the manufacturer's care sheet with the project information. “Wallpaper” is too broad a category for one cleaning method.

### Follow the actual cleaning instructions

Do not assume a natural fibre, silk or metallic surface can be cleaned like a washable vinyl. Water, rubbing or a household cleaning product may change the surface. Use only the method approved for the particular product, including any required testing procedure.

If you are unsure what the material is, ask before cleaning. A small mark is often easier to assess than a larger area altered by an attempted repair.

### Record a problem before touching it

For a lifting edge, bubble or stain, take an overall photograph and a close-up. Note when it appeared, whether anything nearby changed and whether the wall feels or looks damp. Include the material reference and installation date if you have them.

Avoid cutting a bubble or adding adhesive yourself before the cause has been assessed. Similar visible symptoms can come from different conditions, and a repair can make later diagnosis harder.

### Set realistic repair expectations

Some local repairs are possible, but an exact match is not always guaranteed. Ageing, batches, natural variation and the available spare material all matter. Ask whether the repair will remain visible before agreeing the work.

### Keep the handover information

Retain approved spare material where practical, stored as the supplier recommends. Keep records of product references, batch information, care guidance and the terms actually agreed for the project.

If there is an underlying leak or moisture problem, address that before expecting a decorative repair to last. Aftercare should deal with the cause as well as the appearance.
