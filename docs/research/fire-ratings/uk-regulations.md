# UK regulations for wallcovering fire performance: internal linings in non-dwelling buildings

Research notes, agent A (UK regulatory research). Checked on 15 October 2026. British English; paraphrased from government
documents, with only short attributed quotations. Source IDs in square brackets refer to the `sources` list in
`A-uk-regulations.json` (the register at the end of this file repeats them with URLs). Page references give the printed page
first and, where different, the PDF page in brackets.

Scope: wall and ceiling lining provisions that apply to wallcoverings in non-dwelling buildings (hotels, offices, shops,
assembly) in England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, with a short note on dwellings. This file records what the
official documents say. It is not legal advice and it does not say how any particular product or building complies.

### 2.2 Legal status: requirement versus guidance

- Requirement B2 is in Schedule 1 Part B of the Building Regulations 2010. It requires internal linings to adequately resist spread of
  flame over their surfaces and, if ignited, to have a rate of heat release or rate of fire growth that is reasonable in the
  circumstances. "Internal linings" means the materials or products used in lining any partition, wall, ceiling or other internal structure.
  [src-leg-br2010] The ADB prints the same text with no limits on application (PDF p.66, printed p.54). [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- Approved documents are approved and issued under section 6 of the Building Act 1984. Section 7 (the correct provision for the legal
  effect, "Compliance or non-compliance with approved documents") says failure to comply with a relevant approved document does not of itself
  create civil or criminal liability, but may be relied on as tending to establish liability, and proof of compliance may be relied on
  as tending to negative it. Section 7(1A) ("relevant approved document") was inserted by the Building Safety Act 2022. [src-leg-ba1984-s6-s7]
- The ADB's own introduction (PDF pp.5-6) says it gives practical guidance, following it does not guarantee compliance, and other ways of
  complying may be agreed with the building control body at an early stage. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- Regulation 7(1) requires adequate and proper, appropriate materials. Regulation 7(2) (class A2-s1, d0 or A1) applies to external walls and
  specified attachments of "relevant buildings" with a storey at least 18 m above ground, not to internal linings. [src-leg-br2010]

### 2.3 Requirement B2 and Section 6 (printed pp.54-60; PDF pp.66-72)

Rendered pages were inspected for all of these.

- **Intention (p.54).** B2 is met by restricted spread of flame over internal linings; the fabric should make a limited contribution to fire
  growth, including a low rate of heat release. Circulation spaces are singled out because linings there may be the main route of fire
  spread. B2 guidance does not cover smoke and fumes, upper surfaces of floors and stairs, or furniture and fittings. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **Paragraph 6.1 and Table 6.1 (p.55).** The surface linings of walls and ceilings should meet Table 6.1. The table is one "Classification"
  column in EN 13501-1 terms, with three rows and one note: [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]

| Location (as printed) | Classification |
|---|---|
| Small rooms of maximum internal floor area: (a) 4 m2 in residential accommodation, (b) 30 m2 in non-residential accommodation | D-s3, d2 |
| Other rooms (including garages) | C-s3, d2 |
| Other circulation spaces | B-s3, d2 with footnote marker (1) |

- **Footnote 1 (the only note, p.55).** Wallcoverings conforming to BS EN 15102, achieving at least class C-s3, d2 and bonded to a class
  A2-s3, d2 substrate, "will also be acceptable". The marker (1) is printed on the B-s3, d2 entry for circulation spaces; the note
  text does not name a row. So the printed table offers, for circulation spaces, an alternative of a conforming wallcovering of at least
  C-s3, d2 on an A2-s3, d2 substrate. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **s3, d2 meaning.** Appendix B note after B7: s3, d2 means no limit is set for smoke production or flaming droplets/particles (p.139).
  Table 6.1 therefore does not limit smoke or droplets for any row. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **Paragraphs 6.2-6.3 (walls).** A wall includes the internal surface of internal and external glazing (except glazing in doors) and any part
  of a ceiling sloping at more than 70 degrees. It does not include doors and door frames, window frames, architraves, cover moulds, picture
  rails, skirtings and similar narrow members, fireplace surrounds, mantle shelves or fitted furniture.
- **Paragraph 6.4 (lower-performance parts of walls).** Parts of walls in a room may be of lower performance than Table 6.1 but no worse than
  D-s3, d2. In any one room the total area of lower-performance wall lining should be less than half the room's floor area, up to 20 m2 in
  residential accommodation and 60 m2 in non-residential accommodation. This is a wall-only allowance; there is no equivalent for ceilings.
- **Paragraphs 6.5-6.6 (ceilings).** A ceiling includes glazed surfaces, any wall at 70 degrees or less to the horizontal, the underside of a
  gallery and the underside of a roof exposed to the room below. It excludes trap doors and their frames, window and rooflight frames, and
  narrow members such as architraves, cover moulds, picture rails and exposed beams.
- **Paragraphs 6.7-6.12 (pp.56-57).** Fire-resisting ceilings (EI 30) can reduce the need for cavity barriers; rooflights, special applications
  (flexible membranes, PTFE), insulating core panels used internally (sealed joints; class A1 core in kitchens, hot-works areas), and
  cross-references to flame-spread controls elsewhere (escape stairs, firefighting shafts, exposed surfaces above suspended ceilings).
- **Paragraphs 6.13-6.18, Tables 6.2, Diagrams 6.1-6.3 (pp.57-60).** Thermoplastic windows, rooflights, lighting diffusers and suspended or
  stretched-skin ceilings. Not about wallcoverings, except that para 6.13 allows thermoplastic materials not meeting Table 6.1 only in
  these listed applications.
- **Definitions (Appendix A, p.132 and p.136).** "Circulation space": a space (including a protected stairway) mainly used as a means of access
  between a room and an exit from the building or compartment. "Ceiling": the part of a building that encloses a room, protected shaft or
  circulation space and is exposed overhead. "Room": an enclosed space not used solely as a circulation space, including cupboards that are
  not fittings and large spaces such as warehouses. "Residential accommodation" and "non-residential accommodation" (used in the table and 6.4)
  were not found as defined terms in Appendix A as printed. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **Purpose groups (Table 0.1, pp.5-6).** 2(b) Residential (other) covers hotels, boarding houses and hostels; 3 Office; 4 Shop and commercial;
  5 Assembly and recreation. Table 6.1 is not keyed to purpose group. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]

Illustrative reading of the table, not an official worked example (a design team and building control body decide how a space is classified):

| Space | Likely row | Class in Table 6.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel bedroom of 4 m2 or less, if treated as residential accommodation | Small rooms | D-s3, d2 |
| Hotel bedroom larger than 4 m2 | Other rooms | C-s3, d2 |
| Office, shop or assembly room of 30 m2 or less (non-residential accommodation) | Small rooms | D-s3, d2 |
| Office, shop or assembly room larger than 30 m2 | Other rooms | C-s3, d2 |
| Space mainly used for access to an exit, including a protected stairway | Other circulation spaces | B-s3, d2, or a footnote-1 wallcovering of at least C-s3, d2 on an A2-s3, d2 substrate |

### 2.4 Appendix B: how classes are demonstrated (printed pp.138-140; PDF pp.150-152)

These pages are in the "Effective until 1 September 2029" group. Rendered pages were inspected. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]

- **B1-B5** (replaced by the 2025 amendments): a performance classification is demonstrated by a specific test, by design standards, or by an assessment of
  relevant test evidence (assessments follow the extended-application standard, or the principles of BS EN 15725). B2: test evidence must be
  checked for applicability to the intended use; small differences such as fixing method, joints, dimensions, insulation and air gaps may
  significantly affect performance and should be tested or assessed. B5: tests and assessments by organisations with the necessary expertise
  (for example notified bodies or UKAS-accredited laboratories). [src-adb-2025-amendments]
- **B6-B7:** classes A1 to F under BS EN 13501-1; untested products cannot be classified; smoke s1-s3 and droplets d0-d2.
- **B8-B9 (substrates).** The classification achieved in testing is valid only within the direct field of application, that is when the product
  is fixed in end use to a substrate of that class (B8, as replaced in 2025; the 2022 text said "field of application"). Standard substrates include
  gypsum plasterboard (BS EN 520, density 700 +/- 100 kg/m3), calcium silicate board and fibre-cement board. A classification obtained on a plasterboard
  substrate is also acceptable for products bonded to a gypsum plaster end-use substrate. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated] [src-adb-2025-amendments]
- **B10-B13 (thermoplastics).** A thermoplastic is any synthetic polymeric material with a softening point below 200 C (BS EN ISO 306 Method A120).
  B13: a thermoplastic alone used as a wall or ceiling lining cannot be assumed to protect a substrate, so both surfaces must meet the required
  class; if fully bonded to a non-thermoplastic substrate, only the composite's surface rating must. ADB does not say which wallcoverings count as
  thermoplastic materials. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **Appendix F (printed p.183).** BS EN 15102 is listed as "Decorative wall coverings. Roll and panel form [2007 + A1 2011]"; BS EN 13501-1 as [2018].
  The index points BS EN 15102 only to Table 6.1. The BS 476 block still printed in the current edition lists only fire-resistance and smoke-leakage
  parts (8, 20 to 24, 31.1) and is stamped for deletion in 2029. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]

### 2.5 The 2025 removal of national reaction-to-fire classes, in detail

- The official letter of 2 September 2024 says the update "removes the national classification system (BS476) for reaction to fire and roofs" from
  ADB, which provides statutory guidance for new building work, leaving BS EN as "the sole route of specification" for those classifications. It
  separately says the fire-resistance references are removed later, by the 2029 booklet. [src-mhclg-circular-letter-2024]
- Circular 03/2024 para 9 says ADB was revised "to remove the national classification for reaction to fire". [src-circular-03-2024]
- What the 2025 booklet deleted (Vol 2, booklet pp.17-20): the Appendix B section "National classifications for reaction to fire" (2019-edition
  printed pp.137-138, which held paragraph B10 and Table B1 "transposition to national class"); Table B2 for roofs; the BS 476-3, -6, -7 and -11
  entries in Appendix F and the index. [src-adb-2025-amendments]
- What that deleted section said (pre-2025 text, rendered p.138 of the 2022 edition): where a product was lawfully on the market using the earlier
  national system, Table B1 could be used; C-s3, d2 was transposed to Class 1 under BS 476-7; D-s3, d2 to Class 3 under BS 476-7; B-s3, d2 to Class 1
  under BS 476-7 plus a fire propagation index (I) of at most 12 and sub-index (i1) of at most 6 under BS 476-6. A note said a national class could
  not stand in for a classification that specifies s1, d0. [src-adb2-2022-edition]
- The 2019 English edition did not use the label "Class 0" in Table 6.1 or Table B1 (no hit in either edition). The national route sat in Appendix B,
  not in Table 6.1. Table 6.1 and its footnote 1 are word-for-word identical in the 2022 edition (printed p.55) and the current edition, so
  the March 2025 change removed the national alternative without changing the European values. [src-adb2-2022-edition] [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- Official FAQ 18 (GOV.UK, section added 21 February 2025) adds that the same material can achieve different classes in different forms, fixings,
  orientations and substrates; designers should use the field of application in the test report and have variations assessed by competent
  professionals following B1 to B5; treatments that need maintenance to keep a class (for example a fire-retardant coating) should be flagged in the
  Regulation 38 information. [src-gov-adb-faq]
- Regulation 38 and Vol 2 Section 19 (fire safety information) were replaced in the same 2025 amendments (booklet pp.12-16). [src-adb-2025-amendments]

### 2.6 Existing occupied premises (context only)

- Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (England and Wales): the "responsible person" (art 3) must take general fire precautions (art 8) and make
  and keep under review a suitable and sufficient fire risk assessment (art 9). Art 50 provides for guidance and for compliance or non-compliance with
  applicable guidance to be relied on as tending to establish or negate a contravention. [src-leg-rrfso2005] GOV.UK's plain summary lists the
  duties (assess, tell staff, maintain precautions, plan, train). [src-gov-workplace-fire-safety]
- The Fire Safety Act 2021 s.1 extends the Order to the structure, external walls and common parts of buildings with two or more sets of domestic premises, and flat
  entrance doors; the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 came into force 23 January 2023. Neither text read states a requirement to replace internal
  wallcoverings. [src-leg-fsa2021-s1] [src-leg-fse2022-reg1] No official source found in this research states a universal retrospective
  replacement requirement for existing wallcoverings.
- The official Article 50 risk assessment guides (2006, republished) do discuss linings, in legacy vocabulary: [src-fsra-offices-shops] [src-fsra-sleeping]
  - Offices and shops (England and Wales; printed pp.13, 18, 24, 120): consider linings as fuel (examples polystyrene or carpet tiles); remove or treat
    large areas of highly combustible wall and ceiling linings; avoid combustible linings on escape routes; Appendix B "Decor and surface finishes"
    (p.120, rendered) gives Class 0 for circulation spaces and escape routes, Class 1 for all rooms but not escape routes ("heavy flock wallpapers" are listed
    among materials suitable if flame-retardant treated to Class 1), and Class 3 for rooms under 30 m2, adding that the equivalent European
    classification will also be acceptable and pointing to ADB.
  - Sleeping accommodation (hotels, guest houses, B&Bs and similar; printed pp.13, 19, 26, 50, 123): the same Class 0/1/3 text on p.123 (rendered) with Class 3
    for rooms under 4 m2; p.50 says care is needed over decorative finishes and multi-layer decorative systems, "e.g. wall papers and oil based paints".
  - The small/medium and large places of assembly guides carry the same Appendix B text (PDF pp.120-121 and 130-131; text-extracted, not rendered).
    [src-fsra-small-medium-assembly] [src-fsra-large-assembly]
  - These guides say the standards cited are guidance only and the guide does not set prescriptive standards (offices and shops Preface, PDF p.5). No sign was found that they were revised for the 2025 ADB change; the GOV.UK pages were last updated in 2023 and 2024 (status notes and an accessible version).

### 2.7 Building Safety Act 2022 (brief)

The Act's relevance found here is limited to section 7(1A) of the Building Act 1984 (the "relevant approved document" wording) and the Regulation 38 / fire
safety information amendments, which reference the Act. The higher-risk building regime was not examined; its relevance to hotel, office, shop and assembly

### 2.8 Dwellings (short note)

Volume 1 Table 4.1 (printed p.44, rendered): small rooms of at most 4 m2 and dwellinghouse garages of at most 40 m2 D-s3, d2; other rooms and circulation spaces
within a dwelling C-s3, d2; other circulation spaces including common areas of blocks of flats B-s3, d2, with the same BS EN 15102 footnote; para 4.4 limits
lower-performance wall to 20 m2. Common areas of flats therefore sit in the B-s3, d2 row. [src-adb1-2026-consolidated]

## 3. Wales

- **Edition.** Wales has not adopted the English 2019 edition. The GOV.WALES page (first published 27 August 2015, last updated 19 June 2025) links Vol 2 as the
  2006 edition incorporating the 2010, 2013, 2016, 2017 and 2020 amendments "for use in Wales"; the PDF dates from December 2021. [src-wales-adb-page] [src-wales-adb-vol2]
  The Vol 1 PDF was re-uploaded on 5 August 2026 with the same edition label (reason not found). [src-wales-adb-vol1]
- **Table 10, Section 7, para 7.1 (printed p.103; PDF p.113; rendered).** Two classification columns:

| Location | National class | European class |
|---|---|---|
| Small rooms (not more than 4 m2 residential; 30 m2 non-residential) | 3 | D-s3, d2 |
| Other rooms including garages; circulation spaces within dwellings | 1 | C-s3, d2 |
| Other circulation spaces, including common areas of blocks of flats | 0 | B-s3, d2 with note 5 |

  Notes: 1 refers to B2.v; 2 refers to the Appendix E definition of room; 3 says national and European classes do not automatically equate; 4 says s3, d2 means no
  limit on smoke or droplets; 5 says wallcoverings conforming to BS EN 15102:2007, achieving at least class C-s3, d2 and bonded to a class A2-s3, d2 substrate, are
  also acceptable. The Welsh front matter says the 2017 amendment (WG 011/2017, effective 1 September 2017) changed Table 10 for BS EN 15102:2007 wallcoverings. [src-wales-adb-vol2]
- **Para 7.4 (p.104).** Lower-performance wall parts: no worse than class 3 / D-s3, d2; under half the floor area; maximum 20 m2 residential and 60 m2 non-residential.
  Paras 7.2-7.3 define walls and ceilings in the same way as England.
- **Appendix A (printed pp.198-199).** Retains the national system: BS 476-7 classes 1-4, Class 0 defined as limited combustibility throughout or Class 1 with BS 476-6
  indices (I at most 12, i1 at most 6) and described as "not a classification identified in any British Standard test"; para 16 warns that thickness, substrate, colour, form,
  fixings and adhesive may significantly affect a rating. Welsh Appendix A cites BS EN 13501-1:2018. [src-wales-adb-vol2]
- **Amendments.** The slip taking effect 20 December 2025 (circular WGC 004/2025; Building (Amendment) (Wales) Regulations 2025, SI 2025/704 (W.119), made 17 June 2025) concerns
  external walls (metal composite material ban, solar shading, hotels, hostels and boarding houses brought into the 'relevant building' definition, a replacement Section 13) and does not change Table 10. It
  does not apply where a building notice, initial notice or full plans pre-date commencement and work starts before or within six months of 20 December 2025. [src-wales-slip-2025] [src-wales-wgc-004-2025]
- **National classes: Wales is different from England.** The Welsh Government consulted from 17 October 2023 to 9 January 2024. Its response (8 January 2025, questions 26-27) says national
  classifications for reaction to fire and fire resistance will be moved to an appendix of ADB, not deleted, and that any proposal to remove all BS 476 references needs further research
  and a public consultation. WGC 004/2025 says the slip does not implement the move and that it will follow the next revision of ADB Vols 1 and 2. [src-wales-partb-summary] [src-wales-wgc-004-2025]
  No Welsh date for removal was found.
- **Legal instrument.** The Building Regulations 2010 as they apply in Wales, amended by Welsh instruments; Welsh Ministers approve documents under section 6 of the Building Act 1984. [src-wales-wgc-004-2025]
  The Order 2005 extends to Wales (art 3, 8, 9 are marked E+W). [src-leg-rrfso2005]

## 4. Scotland

- **Current guidance.** "Building standards technical handbook: non-domestic - April 2026" (published 9 March 2026), applying to a building warrant submitted on or after 6 April 2026 and to
  work not needing a warrant commenced from that date, subject to transitional provisions in the amending regulations. The January 2025 edition applied from 1 January 2025. [src-scot-nd-apr2026] [src-scot-collection]
  The April 2026 change list for Section 2 (clause 2.0.5, p.97) does not include clause 2.5.
- **Standard 2.5 Internal linings (printed pp.134-136; rendered pp.134-135).** The development of fire and smoke from wall and ceiling surfaces within the area of origin must be inhibited. [src-scot-nd-apr2026]
- **Clause 2.5.1.** "Wall and ceiling surfaces" means the substrate or lining material including any treatment to restrict flame spread, "but excludes any decorative wallpaper or paints". The guidance
  accepts that such wallpaper or paints are not controlled by it, but multiple layers can increase flame spread, so multiple layers are not recommended when redecorating. The same wording is in the 2022 handbook.
  [src-scot-nd-apr2026] [src-scot-nd-2022-html]
- **Table 2.4 (European Classification for reaction to fire of wall and ceiling surfaces; pp.134-135).** There are no "high/medium/low" risk categories; the table is by building group and location:

| Location | Residential care buildings and hospitals | Shops | All other buildings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Room not more than 30 m2 | C [2] | D | D |
| Room more than 30 m2 | B [3] | C | C [4] |
| Unprotected zones | B | B [5] | C |
| Protected zones and fire-fighting shaft [1] | B | B | B |

  Notes: [1] includes any toilet or washroom within a protected zone; [2] at least D in a room of 4 m2 or less; [3] ceilings may be C; [4] at least B in a storage building (Class 1); [5] enclosed
  shopping centre exceptions for shop-front timbers, unit signs and stallboard risers (up to 20 per cent of the shop front area).
  Limitations on higher risk surfaces: in a room, part of the wall may be one class lower where the total area is no more than half the floor area, maximum 20 m2 in residential and 60 m2 in non-residential buildings.
  Annex 2.E (p.264) deems the s3, d2 sub-indices to be included where the handbook gives classes A2, B, C or D without them. [src-scot-nd-apr2026]
- **Assessment (2.5.2).** Wall linings include glazing except in doors and ceilings sloping more than 70 degrees; skirtings, facings, cover moulds, picture rails, doors, frames and fireplace surrounds need not be taken
  into account. Thermoplastics (2.5.3-2.5.7, Table 2.5) follow a TP(a)/TP(b) system and are not for protected zones.
- **National classes.** The linings table and Annex 2.E use European classification only. BS 476 remains in the handbook for fire resistance (Annex 2.D) and for roof coverings (Annex 2.F). When Scotland dropped
  national classes for linings was not established; the 2022 and 2026 tables match. [src-scot-nd-apr2026]
- **Legal instrument and status.** Standards are in Schedule 5 to regulation 9 of the Building (Scotland) Regulations 2004 (SSI 2004/406); the handbook is guidance issued under section 4(2) of the Building (Scotland) Act 2003.
  Clause 0.1.4 (p.23): following it in full should be accepted by the verifier as indicating compliance; alternatives are acceptable if they satisfy the regulations; failure to follow it does not by itself create liability.
  [src-scot-nd-apr2026] [src-leg-bsr-scotland-2004]
- **Relevance to wallcoverings.** Because decorative wallpaper and paint are excluded from the classified surface, the Scottish classification attaches to the substrate or lining system, not the decorative layer. That is a
  feature of the guidance, not a statement that wall finishes have no fire effect, and the multiple-layers warning stays.

## 7. What this does NOT mean

- **Guidance is not the law.** The legal requirement is B2 (England and Wales), Standard 2.5 under Schedule 5 (Scotland) and regulation 34 (Northern Ireland). ADB, the Welsh ADB, the Scottish handbook and Technical Booklet E are guidance whose weight in
  proceedings is set by the statutes (for England and Wales, Building Act 1984 s.7). Other routes may be agreed with the building control body. [src-leg-ba1984-s6-s7] [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
- **"Commercial wallpaper must be class B" is wrong as a summary of England.** Table 6.1 has three rows. Class B-s3, d2 is for circulation spaces; rooms are C-s3, d2; small rooms D-s3, d2. Footnote 1 gives a wallcovering route (EN 15102, at least C-s3, d2, on an
  A2-s3, d2 substrate) as an alternative, as printed on the circulation-space entry. Lower-performance wall area is allowed within paragraph 6.4 limits.
- **Not all four nations use the same table.** The English table has no national column; Wales and Northern Ireland have one. Scotland excludes decorative wallpaper and paint from the classified surface. NI regulation 34(b) is worded differently from B2.
- **The removal of national classes is not shown to be a ban on older products or older test reports.** The documents establish removal from the guidance for new work after the transition. They do not say old BS 476 reports are invalid, and
  existing buildings are not required by these documents to be re-lined. How a BS 476-tested product could demonstrate compliance today is not covered here.
- **Reaction to fire is not fire resistance.** Table 6.1 classes (EN 13501-1) describe surface contribution to fire growth. They give no minutes of resistance and do not make a wall or a room "fireproof"; fire resistance is a separate set of provisions (Appendix B Table B1 (resistance), Section 7 onward).
- **A class belongs to a tested build-up.** B8 and FAQ 18 tie a classification to the substrate and end-use configuration tested; B2 says small differences in fixing, joints and air gaps can matter. A classification for one product or build-up is not a brand-wide
  rating, and nothing here shows a variation is covered unless the classification document says so.
- **It is not a project fire strategy.** Which space counts as a circulation space or a small room, compartmentation, escape design, sprinklers, Regulation 38 information, and any alternative approach belong to the project's fire strategy, designers and the building control body (or the verifier in Scotland). Competent
  persons carry out tests, assessments and the fire risk assessment. These notes do not replace them.
- **The Fire Safety Order does not set a lining class.** It requires the responsible person to assess and manage risk in occupied premises. The Article 50 guides use older Class 0/1/3 wording and example materials; they are guidance and not tied to the 2025 ADB change.
- **Dwellings are different.** Volume 1 has its own table; the common areas of blocks of flats fall in the B-s3, d2 row.

## 8. Conflicts, discrepancies and open questions

Conflicts and discrepancies recorded (none resolved by choosing one source over another):

1. Legacy Article 50 guides (Class 0/1/3, heavy flock wallpapers under Class 1) against ADB England 2025 (no national classes). Different regimes and ages; the guides themselves say the equivalent European class is acceptable. [src-fsra-offices-shops] [src-adb-2025-amendments]
2. England against Wales and Northern Ireland on national classes (removed in England in 2025; retained in Wales and Northern Ireland), and against Scotland on the wallpaper exclusion. [src-adb2-2026-consolidated] [src-wales-adb-vol2] [src-ni-tbe-trackchanges] [src-scot-nd-apr2026]
3. England transition end-date wording: booklet "within the period of six months beginning on that day" against Circular notices "sufficiently progressed before 2 September 2025". Read as the same date; not stated by the Department. [src-adb-2025-amendments] [src-circular-03-2024]
4. Printed cross-reference slips in the English Vol 2: para 6.13 cites B11 and B13 (now B10 and B12) and para 12.22b cites "Section 4" for linings (Section 4 is Small premises; linings are Section 6). [src-adb2-2026-consolidated]
5. GOV.WALES page says last updated 19 June 2025, but the Vol 1 PDF was re-uploaded on 5 August 2026 (HTTP Last-Modified) with the same edition label. [src-wales-adb-page] [src-wales-adb-vol1]

Open questions:

- "Residential accommodation" and "non-residential accommodation" in Table 6.1 and para 6.4 are not defined terms in Appendix A as printed; hotel bedroom treatment needs a design-team and building control decision.
- Whether footnote 1's wallcovering concession was intended for rooms as well as circulation spaces is not stated; the printed marker supports the circulation-space entry only.
- Whether a particular vinyl or plastic wallcovering counts as a "thermoplastic material" (B10) and so engages B13 was not determined.
- Higher-risk building provisions of the Building Safety Act 2022 were not examined; the Building Regulations 2010 notice and transition regulations were read only through circular annexes.
- Scotland: when national classes left the linings table, the transitional provisions behind the April 2026 handbook (the amending regulations it cites, for example the Building (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2025, SSI 2025/312, listed on the collection page, were not read), and Scottish occupied-premises fire legislation were not researched.
- Northern Ireland: AMD 7 and AMD 9 were searched by keyword, not read in full; the SR 2012/192 commencement was taken from the booklet.
- Wales: no revised Vol 2 date was found; Appendix E and the 2013 and 2016 slips were not read.
- BS EN 13501-1 and BS EN 15102 are paywalled and were not read; edition years are as printed in the government documents.
